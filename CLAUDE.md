# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What this is

thirdfold is a browser-based, real-time multiplayer **3D virtual tabletop (VTT)** for tabletop RPGs. It should feel like a living 3D miniature tabletop, not a 2D VTT with a 3D renderer added on. Built so far: rooms (create/join/reconnect) over an authoritative WebSocket server, a 3D table with the connected players (milestone 1), tokens the GM places/assigns/deletes and owners move on the grid (milestone 2), a shared room log with chat, server-rolled dice and system notices (milestone 3), GM-built walls and doors that block movement (milestone 4), fog of war with token vision and GM reveal/hide (milestone 5), saving/loading scenes (milestone 6), which completes the MVP definition of done below, lighting (milestone 7), props with a select/move/rotate/scale/delete editor (milestone 8), 3D dice (milestone 9), and Supabase-backed scene storage (milestone 10). Post-MVP (`docs/ROADMAP.md`), milestone 11 made the opening section of the built-in adventure **The Hollow Bell** playable end to end: the GM starts it, players pick one of four characters, explore Bellweather, talk to an NPC, find a clue, fight a Hollow Hound turn by turn, and walk out to end the section. Milestone 12 made the four characters playable RPG characters: stats, two actions each (a basic attack and a limited ability), status effects, downed and death states, a character sheet and introduction, and GM overrides. Milestone 13 added world objects with states (hidden, visible, interactable, used, disabled, destroyed, moved, opened, closed, lit, unlit) that players change by interacting and the GM can set directly. Milestone 14 made The Hollow Bell a whole story driven by a state machine: nine chapters (village → discover the bell → the monastery gate → the nave → the hidden chamber → the bell rings → descend → the Hollow → the final decision; twelve since M24 added the pit, the waking and the ringing before the decision, and thirteen with M25's `the_descent`, a branch off the decision) across three tables (Bellweather, the monastery, the Hollow), moved on by play alone (talking, finding things, winning fights, walking into places, answering choices), tracking events, clues, NPC states, decisions, fights, defeated enemies, object states and the ending, and saved with the table (scene file v4). Milestone 15 built Bellweather out into a whole village to investigate (the square and well, the Tolling Rest, the Hale house, Widow Crane's cottage, the smithy, the chapel of Saint Agna, the churchyard, the gated mountain path and the pilgrims' arch at its foot) with ten villagers who talk, hand out clues, change state, react to what the party does nearby and take cover from the Hound, plus flickering flames and drifting mist after dusk. Milestone 16 made investigation physical: characters examine, inspect (read), search and interact with things, and listen and observe wherever they stand; some of it takes a server-rolled check (d20 + a stat against a difficulty, one try per character), and what a character finds is evidence (a document, an object, a trace or testimony) known only to them and the GM until their player shares it, while what people say is heard by all; evidence the party knows raises story events that unlock objectives, and people react to what the character talking to them knows. Milestone 17 gave tables elevation and made the monastery thirdfold's 3D showcase: each cell has a level, so there are balconies, ledges, a bell tower and stairs between them; movement climbs one level a step; sight runs in 3D (over walls from high enough, hidden by rising ground) and through windows; players on different levels see each other; and when the party enters the nave the tower bell swings on its chains, dust falls, the table shudders and something huge passes far below. Milestone 18 made interaction physical: characters push, pull, open, close, pick up, drop, rotate, activate, destroy, move and trigger things; pushed and pulled props slide a cell (never through a wall, a token or over a drop), items are carried from table to table and put down anywhere, and a mechanism is a chain of changes the server plays out step by step (pulling the ringing chamber's lever swings it down, runs the chain up the wall, then grinds the grate open, which moves the story on), shown to every client with the same motions and synthesized sounds. Milestone 19 made combat a full loop: when a fight starts everyone rolls initiative on the server, turns go one at a time in that order (a character moves up to its speed and acts once, then ends its turn; each enemy acts on its own turn after a pause), a tracker and an arrow over the token show whose turn it is, and there are three kinds of enemy: the Hollow Hound (rushes and bites), the Bell Cultist (slings from range, knifes up close) and the Bell Keeper (tough, hammers, and tolls its bell to hurt and slow everyone near it), with a new fight against the Keeper and two cultists in the Hollow. Milestone 20 gave the enemies reliable AI, on the server in and out of fights: they only see what the rules let them see (vision, walls, and in the dark only lit cells or right beside them), the Hollow is dark and watched (cultists walk their rounds carrying lanterns, the Keeper stands guard by the Bell) and a fight begins when one of them spots a character; in a fight the Hound chases what it sees, keeps after its prey but turns on whoever hurt it or someone much closer, and runs to where it last saw someone; the Cultist slings from range, knifes up close and falls back when badly hurt; the Keeper never strays far from its post, goes first for anyone near the Bell, tolls when crowded, and tolls every turn once someone has laid hands on the Bell. Milestone 21 made visibility a gameplay system: each player sees only through their own tokens unless the GM shares the party's sight, the GM reveals or hides a whole walled room with one click, a character who walks into a room learns its whole layout (not who stands in it), the GM can hide any token or prop from the players (fog or not), and what each player has discovered is saved with the table and given back by name. Milestone 22 made light a matter of gameplay: a table can have dark areas where only light lets anyone see, even by day (the ringing chamber is one, lit by a single candle stub), characters light and put out torches (in a fight too, as their action), some things are only there while a torch burns (the ringers' carvings in the chamber, a cleft in the Hollow's rock that seals when the cultists' torch goes out), and the Bell's note lights the whole Hollow for a moment (when it is first touched, when the Keeper tolls, and when the chamber's bell rings), for the party and the watch alike. Milestone 23 rebuilt the Hollow as the final environment, a cavern far larger than the monastery (48×36): a black lake running off into the void, a causeway across it to an island walled in ancient stone where the giant Bell hangs in its frame among gears and chains over the pit, the ruins of a chapel on the western shore with a narrow bridge to a ledge by the island's wall (in through the cleft), and terraces climbing the eastern wall to the Steps and the Watch, a shelf of statues with a high bridge down to the island's east arch. Arriving, the Bell sounds and its flash shows the party the whole cavern, whose shape they then remember. Milestone 24 made the Hollow itself the final encounter, in four phases played as chapters: the party discovers it (looking into the pit, unless they already saw its eye), survives its waking (tendrils rise from the pit while the floor cracks under the party and heaves each round), takes hold of the Bell as it starts ringing itself (it hurts, flashes and calls up more tendrils every round nobody pulls its rope; three pulls hold it), and chooses what becomes of it: destroy it (the Hollow's Hand attacks, and only surviving it ends the story), silence it (the Hollow begins to awaken) or use it (speak with the Hollow). Earlier play changes the encounter: a party that already saw the eye skips the pit, a Bell touched before brings one more tendril, Tobin freed with Pell's name helps pull, the hand bell answers the Bell as a pull from anywhere, knowing the ringers' rule shows the way and changes what the Hollow makes of the party, and the promise to Oswin changes a choice and the ending's coda. Milestone 25 made the endings a system of three: Silence (the Bell destroyed, after surviving the Hollow's Hand, or silenced for good), Descent (the party goes down into the pit, a new table, and stills the Hollow's Heart) and Communion (the party rings the Bell and speaks with it); each has its own narration, final scene (the table changes: the Bell broken and every light out, the Bell dark with the eye red below, the whole Hollow lit a calm blue, the Heart still and grey), result (what became of the Bell, the Hollow, Tobin and Bellweather) and completion: the story is over, the table is shown whole to everyone, and nothing in it moves or acts any more. Milestone 26 let the GM direct the adventure instead of building it: a Direct panel pauses the game (players can't move or act, enemies and mechanisms wait), skips to the next scene (winning the fight at hand first), starts any of the story's fights where the party is and ends one as won or calls it off, brings on enemies (joining the fight at hand in initiative order, or standing guard until they spot someone and start the GM's own fight) and removes them, picks people to move, makes any story event happen, sets the time of day and switches lights, reveals and hides areas and rooms, and rolls dice in the open or in secret; with these alone a GM runs The Hollow Bell from start to finish. Milestone 27 made a new player's first session self-explanatory: the invite link asks only for a name ("Join the game"; watching is a link below), then the player chooses a character, is welcomed to where the party is (the server's words, with their first goal), and learns to play by doing it: an interactive tutorial asks them to walk, talk to someone or touch something, look around, open their character sheet and say something or roll, ticking each off as they do it (in any order), then "Start" hands them over to the story; a step indicator shows where they are throughout. Milestone 28 turned that tutorial into onboarding inside the real session, teaching thirdfold's core interaction by using it: move your miniature, look around (Observe notices a faint blue glint up the road), walk up to the glow (marked on the table with a column of light), inspect it, and you've discovered something (a child's tin bell, private evidence, shown with how evidence and sharing work); then talk to someone, open your sheet and say something at the table. Milestone 29 gave thirdfold its sound, all synthesized in the browser and derived from what each client already receives (no audio traffic): ambience for each place and hour (wind and crickets over the village at dusk, the monastery's cold draughts, the Hollow's drone and dripping lake, the Heart beating), footsteps on earth, stone or flesh, doors, dice, combat (hits heavy and light, misses, healing, guarding, fire, the fallen), UI (a click, your turn, a clue, a choice, a new chapter, a refused action) and dynamic music whose mood follows the story (the village, the monastery, the Hollow, the Heart; rising for choices, the dark, fights and the finale; resolving at the ending or mourning a fallen party), crossfading between moods; the bell is its voice: one family of bells built from a real bell's partials rings the great bell's toll, its flash, the Keeper's toll, the hand bell, the music's motif and the UI chimes. A Sound menu in the header mutes it or sets the music, ambience and effects levels. Milestone 30 added a few cinematic moments, camera shots a line of narration calls for, each a turn of the head of a few seconds that any drag, click or scroll ends, leaving the camera with the player: the first bell (the camera looks up the mountain path toward the monastery, which the party then remembers), the bell ring (the tower bell as the party enters the nave), a discovery (the door behind Saint Agna), the Hollow (the camera pulls back over the whole cavern in the Bell's light), and the final encounter (the pit, when Tobin is found and when the Hollow wakes). Milestone 31 made sessions survive normal network trouble and restarts: live rooms are kept (in files, or in Supabase when configured) and restored when the game server starts again, so everyone resumes the same seat with their tokens, the log, the table and the story; a dropped client shows a reconnecting banner (a countdown, the attempt, Try now) and retries at once when the network or the tab comes back; when the GM's connection drops mid-story the game waits for them and carries on when they return; a fight passes the turn of a character whose player has gone after a while; a player who joins a story under way catches up on the ground the party has explored; and the camera view is remembered across a refresh. Milestone 32 made saving a whole session feature: a GM's saves are theirs on any device, by a lasting GM key the server issues to their browser (which they can copy to another), a story in play saves itself as it goes on, and the landing page greets the GM with their latest game ("The Hollow Bell. Last played: Yesterday 21:43. The quiet village · Bellweather. The Warden (Ana). Continue"); Continue opens a new table on that save with the story where it was (chapter, objectives, characters, evidence, what each player had explored), and a player rejoining under the same name gets their character back. Milestone 33 gave the session an end screen: when the story ends, won or lost, everyone sees a headline for the ending ("The Bell is silent"), how the party came through ("The party survived.", "Two of the three survived."), "Adventure complete", who played whom, the time played, the ending, what came of it, the choices made and a tally (chapters, fights won, foes defeated, evidence found); Return to table closes it to keep exploring (a Summary button brings it back), Replay starts the story over with the same party (the GM; a player asks the GM, and everyone sees who would like to play again), and Return to lobby goes back to the landing page. Milestone 34 was a performance pass driven by measurements (`docs/PERFORMANCE.md`: how to measure, what was found, what changed): the server keeps each sight (a token's vision, a light's reach) while the table's obstacles stay the same, so a sync after a move costs a fifth of what it did (the Hollow's view work 10.7 → 2.1 ms with seven viewers, a patrol step 14.3 → 1.5 ms); three.js and the renderer are their own chunk, loaded when a table shows and prefetched on the landing page and join form (the room page 196 → 36 kB gzipped; the join form on slow 4G 1.8 → 1.3 s); the sun's shadows are redrawn only when something on the table changed (a camera move draws about 40% fewer calls), the sun keeps casting in the dark so changing the time of day no longer recompiles every shader, and light is worked out once per frame however many updates came together; a `?perf` overlay shows what rendering costs. Milestone 35 gave thirdfold a repeatable asset pipeline (`docs/ASSETS.md`), with The Hollow Bell as its reference content: sources in `assets/` (models of props, characters, NPCs and enemies made from primitive parts or provided as meshes-only glTF, materials, texture recipes or PNGs, environments that dress a place's floor, ground, walls and table, and sounds rendered from the bell family or provided as WAV/Ogg) build into content-hashed files in `static/assets/` and a checked manifest; every prop, figure and place of the story now comes from it (the village on grass under plaster walls, the monastery's flagstones, the cavern's rock, twelve villagers on seven figures tinted by their colour, the four characters and every enemy as figures), loaded lazily with placeholders until each arrives, and the great and hand bells ring from rendered samples. Milestone 36 made content authoring a structure (`docs/ADVENTURES.md`): an adventure is data (`AdventureDef`: scenes, characters, NPCs, enemies, objects, encounters, events, dialogues, objectives and endings, whose happenings are effects chosen by rules with conditions, the way NPC lines always worked), the engine runs whichever adventure a table plays and knows none of them, The Hollow Bell is written in that structure and runs from it, a validator names every reference that goes nowhere, and a second, tiny adventure plays start to finish on the same engine in the tests. Milestone 37 began user-built tables: the GM starts a new, empty table of a chosen size and look from the Scene panel, paints floors cell by cell or area by area (stone, wood, grass, dirt, sand, water, or off the map, where nobody can stand), and alongside walls, doors, props, ground, lights, fog and tokens, saves it, or shares it: Share table gives a link that opens a copy of the table (the world, without the story or who played whom) for any GM, from the landing page or pasted into the Scene panel. Milestone 38 let creators build adventures without code: an adventure file is the content structure as plain JSON (tables as scene files, hit points as numbers, characters from the library), checked in full by the server, and the builder at `/builder` edits one section by section (scenes, the flow of chapters and objectives, events, people and dialogue, enemies and encounters, things with verbs and rules, trigger areas, clues, choices as branches, rewards, endings), shows what's wrong as you go, saves and opens files, starts from an example (The Miller's Key), and plays it: Play it opens a table with the adventure, and a save carries the adventure with it. Milestone 39 proved the structure is general with a second adventure, **The Last Train to Blackwater**, a supernatural western mystery aboard a night train (Arizona, 1889) written entirely as an adventure file and built in through the same loader: seven chapters over three tables (the train's four cars end to end, the locomotive, the ghost town of Blackwater), six passengers to question, a vanished man, a frost trail, a key carried to the baggage car and its strongbox, the coffins' dead, midnight (the lamps go out and the dead walk the aisles), the dead engineer at the throttle and three endings (Stopped Short, Laid to Rest, End of the Line); the GM picks it from the Adventure panel's catalog of the server's adventures, and its characters are introduced in its own words. Milestone 40 began thirdfold as a platform: creators publish adventures from the builder to a library (`/library`) under a creator name, by their GM key, each publish a new version (games under way keep theirs), and manage them (take one out of the library, put it back, remove it); any GM searches the library by title, story or creator, orders it by rating, plays or date, opens a creator's page, and runs an adventure for their table (from the library or the Adventure panel, which credits its creator); everyone who played it can rate it 1-5 stars once the story is over, and the library counts plays; and a GM can list their game (Invite only / Open to all) so anyone finds it among the front page's open games and joins or watches. Milestone 41 put the rules behind a versioned server-side ruleset contract, with thirdfold's own rules as `thirdfold-classic` v1 (both built-in adventures and every older save play by it, unchanged). Milestone 42 added the fifth edition rules (SRD 5.2.1) as ruleset `dnd-5.5e` v1: six abilities and their modifiers, proficiency by level, skills, ability checks, saving throws and attack rolls as d20 tests, advantage and disadvantage from what the table knows (the dark, an archer with a foe beside it, a target taking cover, an unseen attacker), critical hits and natural-1 misses, and turns with an action and a bonus action; the server works out every number and explains each roll in the log, and a third built-in adventure, **The Barrow on Cold Hill**, plays by it (an Investigation check, an Athletics check, a Dexterity save against a dart trap, a fight in the dark against a Barrow Guard and a Cold Shade whose chill calls for a Constitution save). Milestone 43 imported the SRD 5.2.1 itself into a content catalog: the official PDF is kept in `content/srd/5.2.1/` pinned by its SHA-256, and a deterministic importer (`npm run srd`, checked by `npm run srd:check` and a test) reads it into 947 records (173 rules terms with the 15 conditions, 9 species, 4 backgrounds, 17 feats, 12 classes with every level's features and table columns, 12 subclasses, 38 weapons, 13 armor, 339 spells and 330 monster stat blocks), each with a stable id, its structured fields and text, and its provenance (source, section and pages); every correction made to the source's text is logged, the catalog is validated, and the SRD's CC-BY-4.0 credit travels with the catalog, the rules, every save and exported table of a story played by them, and is shown at the table. Milestone 44 gave the fifth edition rules a character model: a character is a player's choices from the catalog (species and its lineage or ancestry, background and its +2/+1, class, skills, Expertise, Fighting Style, Weapon Mastery, a subclass from level 3, ability scores by standard array, point buy or roll, feats at the levels that grant them, hit points by average or roll, worn armor) plus its state of play, versioned and bound to the rules and the catalog it was made from, owned apart from what it holds; the server checks every choice against the SRD, works out every number (scores, proficiency, saves, skills, Armor Class, initiative, speed, hit points, features, resources from the class table, spellcasting), saves and restores it (migrating older versions, refusing newer ones), and The Barrow on Cold Hill's four characters are built this way. Milestone 45 let players create their own: in a story that allows it (The Barrow does), the character choice offers "Create your own character", a guided six-step creator (class, origin: species with its options and background with its +2/+1, abilities by standard array or point buy, skills with Expertise, Fighting Style and Weapon Mastery, gear: armor, shield and weapons, then name, colour and review) that the server answers with what may be chosen and what the choices come to; a half-made character is kept in the browser and picked up after a refresh; the server builds only a legal level 1 character (no levels, rolled scores or options a page might claim) for a player without one, places its token as theirs, plays its weapons as its attacks (and a Fighter's Second Wind or a Paladin's Lay On Hands), and saves and restores it with the story; the four pregenerated characters are chosen as before. Milestone 46 gave fifth edition characters a full sheet: opened from the action bar or by name in the party list (the GM and every player see the same numbers), it shows what changes in play (hit points, conditions, resources such as spell slots, with pips; ones an action spends are counted by the table, the rest its player or the GM marks used or regained), what the rules work out (initiative, passive Perception, spell save DC and attack, every ability, save and skill with Expertise marked) and what was chosen (species and its options, background, class, score method, skills, Fighting Style, Weapon Mastery, equipment and training, choices kept for later), with every feature and feat's rules text; its player writes notes only they and the GM see, renames a character they built, and all of it is kept with the story through reconnects and saves; the full sheet is sent when opened, not with every update, and the classic characters keep their compact sheet. Milestone 47 gave fifth edition characters their gear: each owns an inventory of SRD weapons, armor and ammunition (the Ammunition table now imported too), every entry with a quantity and where it came from (starting equipment, found on a table, given by someone, from the GM, recovered after a fight) and where it is equipped (armor worn, a Shield, weapons in hand); its Armor Class comes from what it wears and its attacks from what it holds (a Versatile weapon alone in the hands deals its two-handed damage, a weapon its class isn't trained with adds no Proficiency Bonus, empty hands make an Unarmed Strike), a bow or crossbow spends a piece of its ammunition each shot and half comes back once the fight is won; its player equips, puts away, puts down, hands over and picks up things from the sheet and the action bar, the server refusing armor it isn't trained in, more than two hands can hold and more than its Carrying Capacity (Strength × 15 lb.), and in a fight allowing only weapons, on its own turn, twice; what is put down lies on the table in a pile, shown by a prop but kept as the rules' data, not a world object; the GM may give anyone something from the SRD; and all of it survives reconnects, restarts and saves. Milestone 48 gave fifth edition characters their spells: a caster chooses its cantrips and prepared spells from its class's list (the creator's Spells step, checked against the class table: how many, and no level it has no slot for), and sixteen SRD spells are cast at the table (Fire Bolt, Ray of Frost, Shocking Grasp, Chill Touch, Starry Wisp, Eldritch Blast, Sacred Flame, Magic Missile, Burning Hands, Thunderwave, Guiding Bolt, Inflict Wounds, Cure Wounds, Healing Word, Bless and Shield of Faith: spell attacks, saving throws by foes and friends, healing, a cone and a cube from the caster, a push, and concentration), each read from the SRD's own words (a test checks every phrase is in the spell's text) while every other spell sits on the sheet with the reason it isn't cast yet (a condition, a reaction, a longer casting time); a levelled spell spends a slot of its level or one chosen higher (one slot a turn in a fight), slots are kept with the story and regained by hand until rests come, and what lingers (Bless's d4, Shield of Faith's +2, a Ray of Frost's chill, Guiding Bolt's mark, Chill Touch's hold on healing) is a lasting effect on the fight that ends on its caster's turns, with the fight, or when concentration breaks (a Constitution save after damage, or falling); the action bar picks a slot, several targets or an area's aim (drawn on the table), and the sheet lists every spell with its text. Milestone 49 gave the fifth edition rules the SRD's fifteen conditions and one lifecycle for lasting effects: each condition is read from the SRD's own words (a test checks every phrase) and played for what it does (Incapacitated can't act and breaks concentration, speed 0, Advantage or Disadvantage on attacks by and against its bearer, Prone's near-and-far rule and getting up for half a turn's movement, saves failed or hindered, a hit from beside an Unconscious or Paralyzed creature a critical one, a Frightened creature neither closing on its fear nor attacking well while it sees it, a Charmed one sparing its charmer, Exhaustion counted in levels), with what isn't played yet named on it; an effect has a source (a character, an enemy, the GM, the story), a bearer and an end (on its source's turns, a save at the end of its bearer's turns or when hurt, damage, concentration, the fight's end, or the GM), refreshes when the same source applies it again, is suppressed while the same effect from another source applies, stacks as different effects, and is refused where its bearer is immune; spells (Ray of Sickness's poison, Hideous Laughter, Sleep from Incapacitated to Unconscious) and monsters (the Cold Shade's chill frightens) use it alike, the GM puts a condition on anyone or ends one from the Direct panel, and markers with the rules' words show in the action bar, the turn tracker and the sheet; all of it is saved with the story, and a client can't make one. Milestone 50 made fifth edition combat whole: a character's turn has its action, its bonus action and a reaction, and its movement (in cells of 5 feet); the Opportunity Attack is every creature's reaction, taken by the table (a foe that sees a character step out of its reach strikes as it goes, from the cell it leaves, and a character strikes a foe walking away with its melee weapon or an Unarmed Strike unless its player holds its reaction), once until its turn comes round; every character can Dash (its turn's movement again), Disengage (no opportunity attacks this turn), Dodge (attacks it sees have Disadvantage, its Dexterity saves Advantage) and Help (a foe beside it distracted for an ally's next attack, or first aid, a DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) check that steadies a dying ally); a character at 0 HP makes a death saving throw at the start of each of its turns (10 or higher a success, a 1 two failures, a 20 back up with 1 HP, three successes Stable and three failures dead), damage at 0 HP is a failure (two from a Critical Hit) and massive damage kills outright; cover is measured on the grid (lines from the attacker's best corner to the target's corners, walls, doors and props hiding three-quarters, creatures at most half) and adds +2 or +5 to Armor Class and Dexterity saves; damage has its SRD type, halved by Resistance (a Dwarf's poison, a Dragonborn's ancestry, a Tiefling's legacy), doubled by Vulnerability and stopped by Immunity (the Barrow Guard breaks under a mace, the Cold Shade shrugs off cold and fears radiance); every rule quotes the SRD's words (a test finds them in the pinned PDF); the action bar shows each part of the turn, why an action can't be taken now, the reaction (hold or let it be taken) and the death saves, attack entries show the damage taken beside the roll, and all of it (death saves, reactions spent and held, a Dash's movement) is saved with the fight, while the classic rules' fights are unchanged. Milestone 51 brought the SRD's monsters to the table: every stat block is read into an enemy the table plays (313 of the 330: Armor Class, average Hit Points, walking Speed, initiative, saving throws, damage and condition immunities, Resistances and Vulnerabilities, attacks from the SRD's own attack lines with a Multiattack of one attack repeated, damage of a second type on a hit, and a knock Prone), its tactics from what it has (it closes in, or keeps its distance with a ranged attack), and what isn't played yet (traits, spells, breath and other saving-throw actions, conditional riders) named on it, never approximated, with where it comes from (section and page); under the fifth edition rules the GM searches them by name, type or challenge in the Direct panel, places them on the map (held for the GM's own fight, spotting nobody until it starts, or straight into the fight at hand), reads how hard the fight looks by the SRD's own guidance (the XP budget per character by level for Low, Moderate and High difficulty against the monsters' XP, with what it assumes and that it promises no balance), and starts it; the monsters take their turns by the rules (opportunity attacks, damage types and conditions included), and the monsters brought in, those waiting and the fight itself are kept in saves and through reconnects and restarts, while the adventures' own enemies are unchanged. Milestone 52 let creators extend the fifth edition rules with homebrew (`docs/HOMEBREW.md`): a pack is typed plain JSON of weapons, armor, spells (with the mechanics the table casts them by, in the terms the SRD's own spells use) and monsters (written out as SRD stat blocks and read like the SRD's), checked in full by the server against the SRD it pins (its classes, schools, masteries, damage types, conditions and challenge ratings, every field bounded, dice as plain dice, no markup or code, no SRD name taken), and given an id from its content, so the SRD's records keep their ids, words, sources and credit and a changed pack is a new one; the GM adds a pack to a story from the Adventure panel, and from then on that story's character creator offers its gear and spells (marked as homebrew), the GM's Direct panel finds its monsters and the GM can give out its gear, at no other table; a save carries the pack as written and checks it again before anything that uses it, a pack in use can't be put away, and the story records which GM brought it (a public creator id, shared with the table only), ready for collections and grants. Milestone 53 added campaign collections: the library holds three kinds of thing, adventures, homebrew packs and collections, with the same versions, GM-key ownership, listing and plays; a collection names its adventures (ones that come with thirdfold, or library ones at an exact version), homebrew (library packs at an exact version) and shared tables, and the rules they all play by, and holds no copy of them; the server looks each up and checks it as it would be played, and reports every piece as ready, missing, unavailable, doesn't fit (other rules) or broken, publishing a collection only when all are ready; the library page lists collections, opens one with that report and runs it (its first adventure, with its homebrew attached and credited), and a creator publishes homebrew and gathers a collection there; the story keeps the collection and the versions it found, the Adventure panel shows it and lets the GM start its other adventures, and a save names exactly that set again. Milestone 54 put the library behind permissions: every item (an adventure, a homebrew pack, a collection) is public (listed: anyone finds, opens, plays and includes it), restricted (listed, but only its owner and whoever it is shared with may open, play or include it) or private (only they know it is there), and its owner shares it by a grant to a creator (by public creator id: a member plays it, a collaborator also adds versions, puts it in collections and exports what they play), a collection (whoever runs it plays it there) or a table (a room, for at most a day), each grant kept with who gave it and when, running out or revoked but never forgotten; the game server decides every list, read, start, include, publish, manage and export by the GM key, the room and the collection asking, a guessed or forged id answers as if nothing were there, a story keeps the grants it was played by so a revoked one stops its saves from opening and only a collaborator exports, and creators set access, share, revoke and see what was shared with them from the library page, while public content, the built-in adventures and the SRD keep their access. Native targets: `npm run desktop` / `desktop:build` builds Tauri. `npm run android` / `ios` builds, runs `cap sync`, and launches Capacitor. `android/` and `ios/` are generated and gitignored. ## Architecture

Three layers, each importable only in one direction:

- **`src/lib/game/`: shared domain + wire protocol.** Plain TypeScript with no DOM, Svelte, three.js or Node APIs, and relative imports only (no `$lib`), because the game server imports it directly. `grid.ts` holds logical grid ↔ world conversions, `token.ts` the token model, `dice.ts` the dice-expression parser/roller (hand-written tokenizer with hard limits, random source injected), `chat.ts` the log entry types plus `/roll` command parsing (`/gmroll` or `/gr`: a secret roll, whose entry carries an `audience`: the GM only, or the roller and the GM), `objects.ts` walls, windows and doors (they run along grid lines between cells, from corner to corner; `GridEdge`/corner helpers live in `grid.ts`; a window is a wall with `window: true`: it blocks movement, not sight; `windowEdges`) plus the pathing rules (`canStep`, `isReachable` BFS, `findPath`/`walkDistance` shortest walks, `cutWall`); these take `Blockers`: either a bare set of blocking wall/door edges, or full `Obstacles` that add `solid` (can't enter) and `opaque` (can't see past, but the cell itself is visible) cell masks, `windows`, and `levels` (each cell's elevation, or null for a flat table: a step may climb or drop at most `MAX_STEP` = 1 level, so stairs are runs of cells one level apart, and a balcony's edge is a drop nobody steps off), `props.ts` the asset catalog (`ASSETS`: name, footprint, what it blocks) and prop placements (asset id, anchor cell, quarter-turn rotation, visual-only scale), `footprintCells`, `placementProblem` (shared by server and editor preview) and `obstaclesFor(grid, objects, props)`, which is what movement, sight and light should use, `visibility.ts` line of sight (cell-to-cell, blocked exactly where movement is; with levels it runs in 3D from the viewer's eye, its level + `EYE_LEVELS`, to just above the target, + `TARGET_LEVELS`: ground rising above the line blocks it, and a line high enough passes over walls (`WALL_LEVELS` above the higher floor beside them) and sight-blocking props (`PROP_LEVELS`); on a flat table this is exactly the flat rule), round vision radius, `sees` (whether something with a vision radius sees a cell: the same radius and line, and in the dark only lit cells or right beside; what enemies detect with), and `CellMask` ↔ base64 bitset encoding for `FogView`, `lights.ts` the room's ambient level (`day`/`dusk`/`dark`), GM light sources and token-carried light (`lightSources`, `litMask` for the rules, `lightLevels` for rendering; light spreads exactly like sight), dark areas (`withDarkness`; `seenByLight` is the rule: where it is dark, everywhere after dark or in a dark area at any hour, only lit cells can be seen; null means everything counts as lit), `floor.ts` floors (`FLOORS`, append-only: plain (the table's own surface, 0, "Default ground"), stone, wood, grass, dirt, sand, water, `void` (`VOID`): off the map, then cobble, flagstone, rock, mud, snow and gravel (bytes 8-13, #248, rule-neutral like stone), then `tile` and `grating` (bytes 14 and 15, #254, the same, kits tile them); `FloorMap` one byte per cell, `withFloor` (null once all plain), `encodeFloor`/`decodeFloor`, `knownFloor`; `obstaclesFor(grid, objects, props, levels, floor)` makes void cells solid, not opaque), `terrain.ts` the level map (`LevelMap`, one byte per cell, 0 to `MAX_LEVEL`; `withLevel` sets an area and returns null once all flat; base64 `encodeLevels`/`decodeLevels`; `knownLevels` masks it to the cells a viewer has explored), `chat.ts` log entries can carry an `audience` (`'gm'`, or `{ players }`: only those players and the GM read it; `canSeeLogEntry` in `server/views.ts` filters snapshots and broadcasts) and include `check` entries (an investigation check: action, stat, roll, `dc`, success; the client throws the d20); narration may carry a `cue` (`'toll'`), a cinematic moment clients play (presentation only), and a `Shot` (`focus` cell or null for the whole table, `frame` close/wide/table): a camera move; `motion.ts` the `Motion` (a prop's `shake`/`swing`/`land` and a `Sound`: clank, rattle, grind, chime, crack, scrape, thud) sent as the `motion` server message, presentation only, and `heardOnly` (what a viewer who can't see the prop gets: the sound alone); `rooms.ts` finds rooms: `roomBoundary(objects)` (every wall, window and door edge, open or shut) and `roomAround(grid, boundary, cell)`, a flood fill that returns the room's cells or null past `MAX_ROOM_CELLS` (open ground); tokens and props may be `hidden` (GM-set; kept from every non-GM viewer, fog or not, except a token's own owner); `scene-file.ts` the versioned save format (currently v10; the versions and `migrate` in `scene-versions.ts`; upgrades v1 → v2 (lights) → v3 (props) → v4 (`adventure`: the state of a story played at the table, a `SavedStory` `{ id, version, state }` that the core only checks is bounded plain JSON; the adventure module validates the contents) → v5 (`terrain`: the level map as base64, or null for flat; windows on walls) → v6 (`fog.shared`, and `discovery`: each player's explored mask by name) → v7 (`darkness`: the dark areas as a base64 mask, or null) → v8 (`environment`: how the table looks, an environment asset's id, or null; tokens may carry `model`, a model asset's id) → v9 (`floor`: what each cell is made of, a base64 `FloorMap`, or null) → v10 (`world`: the world look from `world.ts`, `defaultWorldFor(ambient)` for older files, and `ambient` always `ambientFor(world, ambient)`; `interior`: roofed cells, checked by `decodeMaskExact`; `discovery[name]` becomes `{ explored, lights? }`, the lights a player remembers, checked by `parseLightList` in `lights.ts`; optional `LightLook`, `TokenLook` and `PropLook` fields, each with its parser)); `world.ts` is the world's look (`WorldLook`, `bandOf`, `canonicalTime`, `ambientFor`, `withBand`, `applyWorldPatch`, `parseWorldLook`/`parseWorldPatch`, `DEFAULT_WORLD`; docs/RENDERING.md "World look"), carried as `room.world` (message `world_set`, GM-only and rate-limited by `lookLimiter`, `setWorld` in `scene-look.ts`; views and snapshots carry `world`, diffs `world_update`; with a sun the band follows the hour: `lookWorld` in `scene-look.ts` (lights and the look's edits, re-exported by `scene.ts`) is the only writer of `room.world` and `room.ambient`, `setBand` for the engine, guarded by `server/ambient-writer.spec.ts`, and `bandNotice` in `game-server.ts` posts one notice per band change) and `room.interior` (roofed cells, presentation only: message `interior_set`, GM-only, `lookLimiter`, `setInterior`; views carry it masked to the explored cells like `darkness`, diffs `interior_update`); `blankScene(name, width, height, environment, now, world)` is a new empty table, and `sharedScene` a table as shared (its look and roofs, no story, no discoveries, no token owners) (`serializeScene`, and `parseSceneFile`, which migrates old versions forward and validates every field; anything read from disk or uploaded goes through it), and `permissions.ts` the role/ownership rules (`canMoveToken`, `canEditScene`, `canUseDoor`: GM always, a player only with an owned token beside the door) that the server enforces and the UI reuses only to decide what to offer. `protocol.ts` defines every `ClientMessage`/`ServerMessage` and `parseClientMessage`, the only way network input becomes typed data; the browser checks what the server sends with `parseServerMessage` in `server-message.ts`, and names and room codes live in `names.ts`, so pages that only talk to the server load none of the parsers.

## Platform constraints

- **The frontend is a static SPA.** It uses `adapter-static` with `fallback: 'index.html'`, and `+layout.ts` sets `ssr = false` and `prerender = false`. It has no SvelteKit server routes, hooks, or form actions. The same bundle ships in the Tauri and Capacitor shells. Anything needing a secret, direct DB access, Redis, or authoritative game logic goes in `server/` (or behind Supabase), never in `src/`.
- `VITE_*` env values are inlined into the bundle at build time. Only the anon/publishable key belongs there, never the service_role key.
- `src/lib/api.ts` rewrites `localhost` to `10.0.2.2` for the Android emulator. Build URLs from `API_URL` / `GAME_SERVER_URL`, not from `import.meta.env` directly. `GAME_SERVER_URL` defaults to port 8787 on the page's host (localhost inside native shells), overridable with `VITE_GAME_SERVER_URL`. `src/lib/supabase.ts` is the shared Supabase client (unused so far).
- Svelte 5 runes mode is forced for all project files (`vite.config.ts` `compilerOptions.runes`). Use `$state`/`$derived`/`$props`, not legacy `export let`/stores syntax.
- Formatting uses tabs, single quotes, and no trailing commas, with a print width of 100 (`prettier.config.js`).

## Product requirements (from the project brief)

The brief sets behavior, not stack. Extend the existing architecture. Don't add frameworks or services unless a concrete need justifies them.

**Layering.** Domain/game state → networking/sync → client state → 3D rendering + UI. The renderer only visualizes state; it is never the source of truth. Keep domain logic (scenes, tokens, grid, movement, visibility, dice, permissions) in plain, renderer-free modules so it can be unit-tested in the `server` Vitest project and reused by whatever runs authoritatively. Keep system-agnostic: no D&D-specific rules in core concepts. The Hollow Bell's own rules (characters, abilities, enemies, adventure state) live apart from the core grid/visibility/permissions modules, so milestone 36 can extract them into content.

**Authoritative server.** Clients send requests. The server validates identity, role, ownership, movement, scene edits, dice expressions, and persisted data, then applies the change and broadcasts it. The client is never trusted for roles, token ownership, positions, dice results, or permissions. Dice are rolled server-side. Dice parsing must never `eval` input. Scene data, chat, and assets must not carry executable content.

**Core concepts.** The domain distinguishes: Room (GM + players), Scene, Scene Object (floor/wall/door/prop/light), Token, Player, Role (GM / player / spectator), and **Grid Position (logical) vs World Position (render)**, which are kept separate. Assets are reusable resources referenced by scene objects, and placement is stored separately.

**Permissions (MVP defaults).** The GM sees everything, moves any token, edits the scene, controls lights, and reveals areas. A player sees allowed areas, moves only owned tokens, rolls dice, and chats. A spectator views, rolls dice, and chats. Enforcement happens server-side, not only in the UI.

**State split.** Shared authoritative state (players, tokens, scene, objects, lights, visibility) is kept separate from local UI state (selection, active tool, camera mode, hovered cell, drag). Don't keep competing copies of shared state.

**Subsystems (MVP level).**

- Square grid only, but behind an abstraction: cell size, dimensions, snapping, highlight, distance.
- Grid-based movement, not physics.
- Tactical and tabletop camera modes. Camera code stays out of gameplay rules.
- Visibility is grid-based: vision radius plus GM reveal, with visible, explored, and hidden states.
- Basic ambient, directional, and point/torch lighting.
- Dice support d4 through d100 and `NdX+M` expressions.
- Room chat carries name, content, timestamp, and type, plus system messages.
- A simple GM scene editor handles select, move, rotate, scale, delete, and creating tokens, objects, walls, doors, and lights. It is not a modeling tool. Primitive placeholder geometry is fine.

**Persistence.** Save plain domain data, never renderer objects. Serialized scenes are **versioned** so the schema can evolve. Reconnecting clients must recover their membership, identity, current scene, and owned-token access.

**Out of MVP scope.** Out of MVP scope: character sheets, a combat system, full rules, marketplace, billing, a complex animation system, procedural generation, advanced 3D line-of-sight, voice/video, campaign management, and mobile-first UX. Don't build these early. The post-MVP roadmap brings some of them in for The Hollow Bell (its four characters, Combat 1.0, cinematic moments). Build them only as far as that adventure needs; the others stay out of scope.

**Performance.** Performance is first-class: no per-frame rebuilds for small changes, instancing for repeated geometry, lazy asset loading, and no high-frequency network traffic for visual-only state. Profile before optimizing. Target modern desktop browsers without requiring bleeding-edge APIs.

**Required test coverage.** Tests must cover grid conversion, movement/distance, dice parsing and results, permission validation, scene serialization, room join/leave, and multiplayer sync of movement, dice, and chat. One automated test must specifically prove **a player cannot move a token they don't control**.
