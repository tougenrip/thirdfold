# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What this is

thirdfold is a browser-based, real-time multiplayer **3D virtual tabletop (VTT)** for tabletop RPGs. It should feel like a living 3D miniature tabletop, not a 2D VTT with a 3D renderer added on. Built so far: rooms (create/join/reconnect) over an authoritative WebSocket server, a 3D table with the connected players (milestone 1), tokens the GM places/assigns/deletes and owners move on the grid (milestone 2), a shared room log with chat, server-rolled dice and system notices (milestone 3), GM-built walls and doors that block movement (milestone 4), fog of war with token vision and GM reveal/hide (milestone 5), saving/loading scenes (milestone 6), which completes the MVP definition of done below, lighting (milestone 7), props with a select/move/rotate/scale/delete editor (milestone 8), 3D dice (milestone 9), and Supabase-backed scene storage (milestone 10). Post-MVP (`docs/ROADMAP.md`), milestone 11 made the opening section of the built-in adventure **The Hollow Bell** playable end to end: the GM starts it, players pick one of four characters, explore Bellweather, talk to an NPC, find a clue, fight a Hollow Hound turn by turn, and walk out to end the section. Milestone 12 made the four characters playable RPG characters: stats, two actions each (a basic attack and a limited ability), status effects, downed and death states, a character sheet and introduction, and GM overrides. Milestone 13 added world objects with states (hidden, visible, interactable, used, disabled, destroyed, moved, opened, closed, lit, unlit) that players change by interacting and the GM can set directly. Milestone 14 made The Hollow Bell a whole story driven by a state machine: nine chapters (village → discover the bell → the monastery gate → the nave → the hidden chamber → the bell rings → descend → the Hollow → the final decision) across three tables (Bellweather, the monastery, the Hollow), moved on by play alone (talking, finding things, winning fights, walking into places, answering choices), tracking events, clues, NPC states, decisions, fights, defeated enemies, object states and the ending, and saved with the table (scene file v4). Milestone 15 built Bellweather out into a whole village to investigate (the square and well, the Tolling Rest, the Hale house, Widow Crane's cottage, the smithy, the chapel of Saint Agna, the churchyard, the gated mountain path and the pilgrims' arch at its foot) with ten villagers who talk, hand out clues, change state, react to what the party does nearby and take cover from the Hound, plus flickering flames and drifting mist after dusk. Milestone 16 made investigation physical: characters examine, inspect (read), search and interact with things, and listen and observe wherever they stand; some of it takes a server-rolled check (d20 + a stat against a difficulty, one try per character), and what a character finds is evidence (a document, an object, a trace or testimony) known only to them and the GM until their player shares it, while what people say is heard by all; evidence the party knows raises story events that unlock objectives, and people react to what the character talking to them knows. Milestone 17 gave tables elevation and made the monastery thirdfold's 3D showcase: each cell has a level, so there are balconies, ledges, a bell tower and stairs between them; movement climbs one level a step; sight runs in 3D (over walls from high enough, hidden by rising ground) and through windows; players on different levels see each other; and when the party enters the nave the tower bell swings on its chains, dust falls, the table shudders and something huge passes far below. Milestone 18 made interaction physical: characters push, pull, open, close, pick up, drop, rotate, activate, destroy, move and trigger things; pushed and pulled props slide a cell (never through a wall, a token or over a drop), items are carried from table to table and put down anywhere, and a mechanism is a chain of changes the server plays out step by step (pulling the ringing chamber's lever swings it down, runs the chain up the wall, then grinds the grate open, which moves the story on), shown to every client with the same motions and synthesized sounds. Milestone 19 made combat a full loop: when a fight starts everyone rolls initiative on the server, turns go one at a time in that order (a character moves up to its speed and acts once, then ends its turn; each enemy acts on its own turn after a pause), a tracker and an arrow over the token show whose turn it is, and there are three kinds of enemy: the Hollow Hound (rushes and bites), the Bell Cultist (slings from range, knifes up close) and the Bell Keeper (tough, hammers, and tolls its bell to hurt and slow everyone near it), with a new fight against the Keeper and two cultists in the Hollow. Milestone 20 gave the enemies reliable AI, on the server in and out of fights: they only see what the rules let them see (vision, walls, and in the dark only lit cells or right beside them), the Hollow is dark and watched (cultists walk their rounds carrying lanterns, the Keeper stands guard by the Bell) and a fight begins when one of them spots a character; in a fight the Hound chases what it sees, keeps after its prey but turns on whoever hurt it or someone much closer, and runs to where it last saw someone; the Cultist slings from range, knifes up close and falls back when badly hurt; the Keeper never strays far from its post, goes first for anyone near the Bell, tolls when crowded, and tolls every turn once someone has laid hands on the Bell. Milestone 21 made visibility a gameplay system: each player sees only through their own tokens unless the GM shares the party's sight, the GM reveals or hides a whole walled room with one click, a character who walks into a room learns its whole layout (not who stands in it), the GM can hide any token or prop from the players (fog or not), and what each player has discovered is saved with the table and given back by name.

## Commands

```bash
npm install
cp .env.example .env        # set VITE_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY from `npx supabase start` output
npm run db:start            # local Supabase (API :54321, Postgres :54322, Studio :54323); db:stop
npm run cache:up            # Redis :6379 via docker compose; cache:down
npm run server              # authoritative game server, ws://localhost:8787 (tsx watch); saves scenes to ./data/scenes (SCENES_DIR), or to Supabase with SUPABASE_URL + SUPABASE_SERVICE_KEY
npm run dev                 # web on http://localhost:1420 (strictPort)

npm run check               # svelte-check for the app + `tsc -p server` for the game server
npm run lint                # prettier --check + eslint
npm run format              # prettier --write
npm test                    # all vitest projects, run once
npm run build               # static SPA into build/
```

Tests are split into two Vitest projects in `vite.config.ts`:

- `server` runs in Node and picks up `src/**/*.{test,spec}.ts` plus `server/**/*.{test,spec}.ts`. Put pure domain logic tests here (grid, dice, permissions, serialization). `server/game-server.spec.ts` starts a real server on port 0 and drives it with `ws` clients; extend it for every new multiplayer action.
- `client` runs in headless Chromium through Playwright and picks up `src/**/*.svelte.{test,spec}.ts`, the component tests.

Run a subset:

```bash
npx vitest run --project server
npx vitest run src/lib/path/to/file.spec.ts
npx vitest run -t "test name"
```

`expect.requireAssertions` is on, so a test with no assertions fails. The live Supabase tests in `server/supabase-scene-store.spec.ts` are skipped unless `SUPABASE_URL`, `SUPABASE_SERVICE_KEY` and `SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` are set; to run them, start local Supabase (`npm run db:start`, which needs a Docker daemon), apply migrations (`npx supabase migration up`), and export the values from `npx supabase status -o env` (`API_URL`, `SECRET_KEY`, `PUBLISHABLE_KEY`). The `client` project needs a Playwright Chromium that matches the installed `playwright` version.

CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs on every pull request and on pushes to `main`, with two jobs. `verify` runs `npm ci`, installs Playwright's Chromium, then `check`, `lint`, `test` (both projects) and `build`. `supabase` starts a local Supabase with the migrations applied (`npx supabase start`, only the services the tests need), exports `SUPABASE_URL`/`SUPABASE_SERVICE_KEY`/`SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` from `supabase status -o env`, and runs `server/supabase-scene-store.spec.ts`, failing if any test was skipped. A change that needs new env for tests must wire it into the workflow, or the tests will silently skip there.

Schema changes: `npx supabase migration new <name>`, write SQL in `supabase/migrations/`, then `npx supabase db reset`. This drops local data. RLS is on for new tables, so a table without a policy returns empty results.

Native targets: `npm run desktop` / `desktop:build` builds Tauri. `npm run android` / `ios` builds, runs `cap sync`, and launches Capacitor. `android/` and `ios/` are generated and gitignored. Recreate them with `npx cap add <platform>`. Native builds only pick up web changes after `npm run mobile:sync`.

## Architecture

Three layers, each importable only in one direction:

- **`src/lib/game/`: shared domain + wire protocol.** Plain TypeScript with no DOM, Svelte, three.js or Node APIs, and relative imports only (no `$lib`), because the game server imports it directly. `grid.ts` holds logical grid ↔ world conversions, `token.ts` the token model, `dice.ts` the dice-expression parser/roller (hand-written tokenizer with hard limits, random source injected), `chat.ts` the log entry types plus `/roll` command parsing, `objects.ts` walls, windows and doors (they run along grid lines between cells, from corner to corner; `GridEdge`/corner helpers live in `grid.ts`; a window is a wall with `window: true`: it blocks movement, not sight; `windowEdges`) plus the pathing rules (`canStep`, `isReachable` BFS, `findPath`/`walkDistance` shortest walks, `cutWall`); these take `Blockers`: either a bare set of blocking wall/door edges, or full `Obstacles` that add `solid` (can't enter) and `opaque` (can't see past, but the cell itself is visible) cell masks, `windows`, and `levels` (each cell's elevation, or null for a flat table: a step may climb or drop at most `MAX_STEP` = 1 level, so stairs are runs of cells one level apart, and a balcony's edge is a drop nobody steps off), `props.ts` the asset catalog (`ASSETS`: name, footprint, what it blocks) and prop placements (asset id, anchor cell, quarter-turn rotation, visual-only scale), `footprintCells`, `placementProblem` (shared by server and editor preview) and `obstaclesFor(grid, objects, props)`, which is what movement, sight and light should use, `visibility.ts` line of sight (cell-to-cell, blocked exactly where movement is; with levels it runs in 3D from the viewer's eye, its level + `EYE_LEVELS`, to just above the target, + `TARGET_LEVELS`: ground rising above the line blocks it, and a line high enough passes over walls (`WALL_LEVELS` above the higher floor beside them) and sight-blocking props (`PROP_LEVELS`); on a flat table this is exactly the flat rule), round vision radius, `sees` (whether something with a vision radius sees a cell: the same radius and line, and in the dark only lit cells or right beside; what enemies detect with), and `CellMask` ↔ base64 bitset encoding for `FogView`, `lights.ts` the room's ambient level (`day`/`dusk`/`dark`), GM light sources and token-carried light (`lightSources`, `litMask` for the rules, `lightLevels` for rendering; light spreads exactly like sight), `terrain.ts` the level map (`LevelMap`, one byte per cell, 0 to `MAX_LEVEL`; `withLevel` sets an area and returns null once all flat; base64 `encodeLevels`/`decodeLevels`; `knownLevels` masks it to the cells a viewer has explored), `chat.ts` log entries can carry an `audience` (`'gm'`, or `{ players }`: only those players and the GM read it; `canSeeLogEntry` in `server/views.ts` filters snapshots and broadcasts) and include `check` entries (an investigation check: action, stat, roll, `dc`, success; the client throws the d20); narration may carry a `cue` (`'toll'`), a cinematic moment clients play (presentation only); `motion.ts` the `Motion` (a prop's `shake`/`swing`/`land` and a `Sound`: clank, rattle, grind, chime, crack, scrape, thud) sent as the `motion` server message, presentation only, and `heardOnly` (what a viewer who can't see the prop gets: the sound alone); `rooms.ts` finds rooms: `roomBoundary(objects)` (every wall, window and door edge, open or shut) and `roomAround(grid, boundary, cell)`, a flood fill that returns the room's cells or null past `MAX_ROOM_CELLS` (open ground); tokens and props may be `hidden` (GM-set; kept from every non-GM viewer, fog or not, except a token's own owner); `scene-file.ts` the versioned save format (currently v6; `migrate` upgrades v1 → v2 (lights) → v3 (props) → v4 (`adventure`: the state of a story played at the table, a `SavedStory` `{ id, version, state }` that the core only checks is bounded plain JSON; the adventure module validates the contents) → v5 (`terrain`: the level map as base64, or null for flat; windows on walls) → v6 (`fog.shared`, and `discovery`: each player's explored mask by name)) (`serializeScene`, and `parseSceneFile`, which migrates old versions forward and validates every field; anything read from disk or uploaded goes through it), and `permissions.ts` the role/ownership rules (`canMoveToken`, `canEditScene`, `canUseDoor`: GM always, a player only with an owned token beside the door) that the server enforces and the UI reuses only to decide what to offer. `protocol.ts` defines every `ClientMessage`/`ServerMessage` and `parseClientMessage`, the only way network input becomes typed data.
- **`server/`: the authoritative game server** (Node + `ws`, run with `tsx`, own `server/tsconfig.json`). `rooms.ts` is socket-free room state (`RoomManager`, returns `Result` values with an `ErrorCode`). `scene.ts` holds the token and wall/door actions (`createToken`, `moveToken`, `createObject`, `toggleDoor`, `setTerrain` (GM, message `terrain_set`: an area to a level), …), and `obstacles(room)` (walls, props and `room.terrain`), each taking the acting `Player` and checking permission and validity before mutating. Player moves must be reachable without crossing walls or closed doors; GM moves are exempt. A door placed on a wall cuts that wall (`objects_changed` carries the resulting upserts and removals together). `views.ts` is where fog is enforced: `viewFor(room, viewer)` returns the tokens, objects, `FogView` and ground (`terrain`: `room.terrain`'s levels, only for cells the viewer has explored under fog; `terrain_update` carries changes) a viewer may see (fog off: everything; GM: everything plus the party's visibility for shading; player: own tokens' vision (the whole party's when `fog.shared`, set by the GM with `fog_share`) ∪ GM-revealed cells, where in `dark` ambient a token only sees cells that some light reaches (plus its own cell), computed once per sync in `sceneContext`; plus remembered `explored` cells, which get walls, props and light fixtures but not tokens, and a token standing in a walled room marks the whole room explored (its layout, via `SceneContext.rooms`); `fogRoom` (message `fog_room`) reveals or hides the room around a cell, refusing open ground; spectator: the whole party's vision). `game-server.ts` never broadcasts scene changes directly: after each scene action `syncRoom` recomputes every connected viewer's view and sends only `diffView` against what that socket was last sent (`sentViews`), so hidden tokens, walls and their ids never reach a client (a test inspects raw frames for this). Log entries can carry `audience: 'gm'` (notices naming GM-only tokens) and are filtered per viewer too. Scenes: `scene-io.ts` exports a room to a scene file and applies one back (replacing grid/tokens/walls/fog, re-matching token owners by id then by player name, restoring each player's explored map by lowercase name from `discovery`, else empty); `scene-store.ts` stores them by unguessable 128-bit id (`FileSceneStore`, atomic write-then-rename, ids validated before touching paths; `MemorySceneStore` is the default for tests); `supabase-scene-store.ts` is the Postgres-backed store (table `public.scenes` from `supabase/migrations`, RLS on with no policies or grants for `anon`/`authenticated`, so only the server's service/secret key can touch it). `server/index.ts` picks Supabase when both `SUPABASE_URL` and `SUPABASE_SERVICE_KEY` are set, else files; setting only one is an error. Save/load/import/export are GM-only, rate-limited, async, and report storage failures as `persistence_failed`. A load sends every viewer a fresh `room_reset` snapshot and restarts their diffs. The GM's browser remembers its save ids in `localStorage` (`thirdfold:scenes`); that list is the only index of saves. `chat.ts` appends to the room log (`room.log`, capped at `LOG_LIMIT`, sequenced by `room.nextSeq`, included in snapshots) and rolls dice with `crypto.randomInt` (`startGameServer({ rollDie })` overrides it). Chat and dice go through a per-player `RateLimiter`. System notices (joins, token placed/moved/reassigned/removed) are posted by `game-server.ts` after a successful action. `game-server.ts` is transport: parse frame → entry messages go to `RoomManager`, in-room messages to `scene.ts` → reply with an `error` to the sender or broadcast the result to the room (rejected actions are never broadcast). State is in memory; rooms are pruned after 10 min with nobody connected. A new game action is: message + parser case in `protocol.ts`, rule in `scene.ts` (with unit tests), a case in `handleInRoom`, a fold in `room-state.ts`, and a multi-client test in `game-server.spec.ts`. Its `TestClient` queues `chat` messages separately from state messages, so `expect('token_moved')` is unaffected by notices; use `expect('chat')` to read the log stream. Log entries must be announced in `seq` order, because clients drop an entry older than one they already have: post an action's notice before running story hooks that add to the log (`token_move`, `token_delete`). `applyOutcome` also sends an outcome's `motions` (after the sync, per viewer: the whole motion if that socket was sent the prop, else only its sound, so hidden prop ids never go out) and schedules its `mechanisms` steps (`runMechanism` after each step's delay times `mechanismDelayScale`; tests pass 0).
- **Client (`src/lib/net`, `src/lib/tabletop`, `src/routes`).** `RoomConnection` (`room-connection.svelte.ts`) owns the socket and exposes `$state` (`status`, `room`, `playerId`, `error` for connection failures, `actionError` for rejected actions). The server's snapshot is the client's shared state. Broadcasts are folded in by the pure `applyRoomUpdate` in `room-state.ts`. Components must not mutate `conn.room`; they call `conn.send(action)` and wait for the broadcast (no optimistic updates). `ui/RoomView.svelte` owns local UI state (selection, placing tool, hovered cell, camera view) and turns clicks into actions. `tabletop/renderer.ts` is imperative three.js (render on demand, no per-frame loop when idle). It receives grid/tokens/objects/selection/highlight/editor previews and reports clicks and hovers as a `Pick` (cell, corner, nearest edge + distance, token id, object id) via `TabletopEvents`, so nothing above it handles world coordinates. `tabletop/tokens.ts` diffs tokens by id and tweens moves; `tabletop/walls.ts` draws all wall units as one `InstancedMesh` and doors as hinged meshes that swing, 3D dice are presentation only: `tabletop/dice-throw.ts` maps a server `DiceRoll` to the dice to show (d100 as tens + units; unmodelled dice like d7 are skipped; capped at 12) and seeds the throw from the roll's log `seq` so every client sees the same throw; `tabletop/dice-geometry.ts` builds the polyhedra (the d10 is a hand-built trapezohedron) and finds faces by grouping triangles by normal; `tabletop/dice3d.ts` animates a scripted arc with bounces that settles on `landingQuaternion` for the rolled face (the d4 rests on the face opposite its result vertex and is read at the top). Dice are driven by the wall clock (`tick(now)`), not by summed frame steps, so they land when `RoomView` shows the result card even on slow frames; a test reads the landed number for every face of every die. With elevation, `tabletop/ground.ts` turns levels into heights (`STEP_HEIGHT`; `WALL_HEIGHT` is `WALL_LEVELS` steps, so the picture matches the sight rule) and every layer stands on it: `tabletop/terrain.ts` draws raised cells as one `InstancedMesh` of boxes (shaded by instance colour for fog and darkness, since the flat overlays lie under them), tokens, props, lights and highlights sit on their cell's floor, walls reach from the lower floor beside them to a wall above the higher one (a window is a sill and, between equal floors, a lintel), and picking hits raised cells before the table. `tabletop/effects.ts` plays cues (`playCue`): the toll swings the bell (`PropLayer.setSwing`, parts marked `swings` rotate about `SWING_PIVOT`), sifts dust, shudders the camera and slides a shadow across the floor, on the wall clock; reduced motion keeps only a gentle swing. `RoomView` plays the latest cue among new log entries. `tabletop/props.ts` draws props as one `InstancedMesh` per asset part (looks defined as primitive parts in `tabletop/prop-models.ts`, separate from the rules), with instance colours for the selection/hover tint; a prop that moves or turns glides there, and `animate` plays a motion (shake, swing of the parts marked `swings` about the asset's `SWING_PIVOTS`, land), all on the wall clock (`tick(now)`) and only while something moves; `playMotions` on the renderer also plays each motion's sound (`tabletop/sounds.ts`, synthesized with Web Audio, a stand-in until M29); `tabletop/lighting.ts` applies the ambient preset, a smooth darkness overlay from `lightLevels`, lantern fixtures, and a fixed pool of 8 real point lights assigned to the strongest sources (fixed so shaders never recompile), and `tabletop/ambience.ts` draws low mist at dusk and after dark (a few soft planes under the darkness and fog overlays, clipped to the table, so it only shows where the viewer can see) and `LightingLayer.flicker` makes flames waver; these two redraw on a slow timer (`AMBIENT_FRAME_MS`, not every frame) and are off under `prefers-reduced-motion`; `tabletop/fog.ts` darkens the floor from the `FogView` with a one-texel-per-cell `DataTexture` (players: black/dim; GM: light tint of what the party can't see) (all cosmetic). The GM's build tools (`ui/BuildPanel.svelte`: select/wall/door/erase V/W/D/E, plus fog on/off and reveal/hide area tools R/H, reveal/hide room tools O/K (hovering previews the room), a shared party sight switch, ambient Day/Dusk/Dark, the ground tool G (click two corners to set an area to a level), the light tool L and the prop tool P; with Select, the GM clicks a prop to select it, clicks a cell to move it, `[`/`]` rotate, Delete removes, and `ui/PropInspector.svelte` also scales it and hides it from players; `TokenPanel` hides a token; the GM sees hidden tokens see-through and hidden props pale) are local UI state in `RoomView`. `Tabletop.svelte` just mounts the renderer and forwards props.

The adventure (The Hollow Bell) is kept apart from the system-agnostic core. `src/lib/adventure/` is the shared, public part (relative imports only): `adventure.ts` also holds the investigation vocabulary (`InvestigationAction` and `INVESTIGATION_ACTIONS`: examine, inspect, search, listen, observe, interact; `Sense` = listen/observe; `EvidenceKind` document/object/environment ("Trace")/testimony; `Check` = stat + dc; a `Clue` in the view carries its kind, who found it, whether it is `shared`, and whether it is `mine`), `CHAPTER_IDS`/`ChapterId` and `LOCATION_IDS`/`LocationId`, and `AdventureStage` is now coarse (`choosing`/`playing`/`complete`/`defeat`); where the party is lives in `chapter` and `location`. `characters.ts` (the four characters: hp/armor/speed, `stats`, an `intro`, and `actions` (the first is the basic attack; `kind` attack/heal/guard, `target` enemy/ally/self, `range`, governing `stat` (attack bonus = 2 + stat, `toHitFor`), `dice`, an optional status it `applies`, `uses` per encounter or null), `STATUSES` (guarded +2 armor, slowed half speed, burning 1d4 at the start of the enemy's turn), `BLEED_OUT_ROUNDS`, `defenseFor` = 10 + armor, `describeAction`) and `adventure.ts` (`AdventureView`, the per-viewer wire shape, `ObjectState`/`OBJECT_STATES` and `ObjectKind`, plus the reach rules `canReach`/`inAttackRange`/`inActionRange` that the server enforces and the UI uses to offer buttons). `server/adventure/` is authoritative and server-only, so undiscovered story never reaches a client (never import it from `src/`): `story.ts` is the state machine as data: `CHAPTERS` (title, location, objectives each `done` by an event and shown `after` one, and `next: { on: EventId, to }`), `transition`, `objectivesFor` (the objectives of the chapters so far at the current location), `EventId`/`EVENT_IDS`, `NPCS` and their states, `DECISIONS` (the promise to Oswin, what to do with the Bell), `EncounterId`s and `ENDING_FOR`. `tables.ts` has small builders for scene files; `bellweather.ts` (36×28: the village; spawn on the valley road at the south edge), `monastery.ts` (30×20, the showcase of height: the courtyard and gatehouse, the nave with windows onto the courtyard and the gallery (`GALLERY`, a balcony at level 5 behind a see-through railing, with a stair down), the outside stair up the east wall to the `LEDGE` and the ringers' door, the bell tower's stair to the `BELFRY` at level 10 with its bell (`BELL_AT`) and open arches, the brothers' ledgers, the sealed ringing chamber behind `SECRET_EDGE`, the grate over the `STAIR`; `TERRAIN` built with `stair()` from `tables.ts`) and `hollow.ts` build the three tables (fixed ids in `IDS`/`MONASTERY_IDS`/`HOLLOW_IDS`, spawns, areas); `locations.ts` maps each `LocationId` to its scene and spawn, and lists `AREAS` (cells that raise an event when a character walks in, only `during` one chapter). `npcs.ts` holds the people (`NPCS`, 12 across the story, 10 in the village: token id, `location`, `home`, `places` (`calm`, and optionally where they go `hiding` while the well's Hound is loose and `after` it dies), `states`, and `lines`: each line has conditions (`if`: the speaker's state, clues found, events that have or have not happened, a pending choice, world object states) and effects (a `clue`, the speaker's new state `becomes`, a story `event`, `heals` the standing party); `once` lines are said once, and the last line always applies), `npcTokens(location)` for the scenes, and `REACTIONS` (a line someone calls out on an `object:verb` within earshot, or on `event:<id>`, once each). `signs.ts` lists `SIGNS`: evidence that is part of a place (the well's hum, small footprints under the north gate, the shrine's vigil, the trail in the nave's dust, the hollow wall, warm air from the grate, the eye in the pit), each picked up by `listen` or `observe` within `range` cells with a clear line, behind a `check`. `content.ts` holds the rest of the text (clues as `ClueDef` with a `kind` and optionally the event they `unlocks`, narration, read-aloud cues, `ENDINGS` and the promise codas, the Hound's stats), `objects.ts` the world objects (`OBJECTS`: each is a token, prop or door in the scene at one `location` (every NPC is one too, generated from `NPCS`, with the verb `talk`; a verb's investigation `action` defaults from its id (`actionOfVerb`: examine→examine, read→inspect, search→search, anything else→interact), and a verb may need a `check`), with a `kind`, an `initial` state, the `states` the GM may set, `verbs` available per state with the state they lead `to`, per-state `looks` (a prop's asset or offset from its scene position, a door's open, a light's on) applied by `applyLook`, and a `disabledText`; a `secret` door is a wall named `<id>-sealed` while hidden and becomes the door when found), `state.ts` the `AdventureState` on `room.adventure` (stage, chapter, location, events in order, defeated enemy names, NPC states, decisions made and the one `pending`, each encounter's state, the ending; `said`: lines said (`<npc>:<line>`) and reactions heard (`reaction:<id>`); `evidence` (by clue id, in the order found: the characters who found it themselves `by`, and whether it is `shared` with the party; `knows(adventure, character, clue)`), `tried` (failed checks, `<character>:<object>:<verb>` or `<character>:sign:<id>`: one try each per character); per character: hp, statuses with rounds left, uses this encounter, `downedFor`, `dead`; clues; each world object's state and where its prop started (`origins`); the encounter, whose enemies have statuses too), `engine.ts` the rules, `persist.ts` `saveAdventure`/`readAdventure` (the story as plain JSON in the scene file, read back and validated field by field against the scene it was saved with before the room is touched), and `view.ts` `adventureView` (enemies only if their token is in the viewer's view; interactables (non-hidden objects with verbs for their state) only once their cells were seen; everyone gets the chapter, location, pending decision, decisions made and ending; cues, the object list of the current location and the `ledger` (events, NPC states, fights, defeated) GM-only). Hidden objects' props are dropped from every non-GM view in `viewFor` (`hiddenPropIds`), fog or not, so a secret never reaches a player until the story or the GM reveals it. `interact(room, actor, objectId, verb)` checks the verb is allowed in the object's state, moves it to the verb's state (the look follows), (a verb with a `check` first rolls it in `rollCheck`, logged for everyone; a failure records the try and tells only that player they found nothing) then `respond` runs the story for `object:verb` (what an investigation finds goes to `addClue` with a finder: its text only to that player, a public "X found something (kind)" notice for the rest; the well's scratches, which everyone sees, and what people say go in with no finder, shared at once; evidence the party comes to know raises its `unlocks` event through `happen`, which is how optional objectives like "Find out where Tobin went" and the monastery's "Find what Saint Agna holds" open; talking goes to `talk`, which picks the NPC's first applicable line and applies its effects; `react` then lets nearby people call out, to the finder only when the verb was investigating, so a reaction never gives a private find away; NPC lines' `clues` conditions check what the character talking knows; narration, clues, story events; lifting the rug reveals the hidden floorboard, turning Saint Agna reveals the secret door, the Hound leaves its `remains` where it dies). Entering the nave tolls the bell (narration with `cue: 'toll'`), and so does the chamber's bell ringing. A chapter whose event already happened is passed straight through. After the final decision `end` sets the ending, `stage: 'complete'` and `completedAt`. `afterMove` raises area events. `settlePeople` moves villagers to their `hiding` places when the well fight starts and to their `after` places when it is won (only when the cell is free; tokens tween like any move). `sense` (message `adventure_sense`) lets a character listen or observe: every sign of that sense in range that it doesn't know and hasn't tried gets its own check; `share` (message `adventure_share`) lets the finder (or the GM) tell the party, posting the evidence for all. Both, and interacting, are refused mid-fight. `decide` (message `adventure_decide`) answers the pending choice: any player with a standing character, or the GM, and the first answer stands; it is refused mid-fight. Doors are world objects too: `doorLock` refuses a `disabled` door (the gate until the Hound falls, the ringers' door until Oswin is answered, the monastery's great doors) to players, `afterDoorToggle` keeps the object's state in step. The GM's `setObject` (message `adventure_object`) puts any object at the current location in any of its states, with a GM-only notice; it does not raise story events. Physical interaction: a verb may say what it physically does (`physical`, a `Physical` from `src/lib/adventure/adventure.ts`: push, pull, open, close, pick_up, drop, rotate, activate, destroy, move, trigger), a `sound`, and an item it `needs`. `interact` works out a move in `plan` before any check is rolled: pushing slides the prop a cell away from a character standing square to it, pulling slides it toward the character, who steps back (`fits`: in bounds, free of solid things and tokens, no wall or drop crossed); turning rotates it a quarter; these move the object's origin (`Origin` is pos, asset and rotation), which its look is applied on. An object with `carry` is an item: picking it up puts it in `AdventureState.carried` (state `carried`, its prop off the table, only its carrier sees it among `interactables` (`carried: true`, no cells) and in `CharacterStatus.carrying`), it travels with the party (`travel` keeps carried items' origins), dropping puts it down where the carrier stands, and a removed character's items fall where it stood. `motionFor` gives each verb its motion and sound. `mechanisms.ts` lists `MECHANISMS` (steps: `after` ms, an object `set` to a state, a `motion` on a prop, `text`, an `event`) and `TRIGGERS` (`<object>:<verb>` → mechanism); `startMechanism` runs the first step at once and records the next in `AdventureState.running`, and `runMechanism(room, id, step)` (called by the game server; a stale or repeated step does nothing) runs the rest; a save keeps `running` and loading schedules it again (`pendingMechanisms`). The monastery's physical things: the ringing chamber's lever (`LEVER_AT`, triggers `grate`: the chapter `descend` opens with the grate slammed shut, and walking onto the stair (`Area.after`) only counts once `opened_grate` happened), the chamber's crate (push or drag it; moving it the first time finds Tobin's tally marks), the gatehouse hand bell (take it, ring it (steadies the party once), put it down), the chains on the great doors (break them with a Might check to open the doors from inside), Saint Agna (turns a quarter), plus the chest's open/close, the rug (move), the crate (destroy), the brazier (activate). A character's player is whoever owns its token, so GM reassignment/removal just works. Encounters (Combat 1.0): `startEncounter` spawns the foes (`ENCOUNTERS` in `engine.ts`: the well's Hound; the chamber's two weaker Hounds and a Cultist; the Hollow's Bell Keeper and two Cultists, already on the table as sentries, when one of them spots someone), rolls initiative with the room's dice (`room.dice`, set by the game server to its `rollDie`; engine tests set it; d20 + Agility for characters, + the kind's `initiative` for enemies; highest first, characters first on a tie) into `Encounter.order`, and `advance`s to the first turn. `current` indexes whose turn it is and `turn` is bumped on every turn. A character's turn: at its start its statuses count down (`tick`; a slow still halves this turn's `speed`), and a downed character bleeds instead (`downedFor`, dead at `BLEED_OUT_ROUNDS` of its turns) and is skipped; on its turn it moves up to `speed` (walking distance, charged in `afterMove`) and acts once (`act` with an action id and a target token), and the turn ends with `endTurn`, by itself once it has acted and walked its full speed, or when the GM ends it (`control` `end_turn`, which also ends an enemy's turn); outside a fight only healing actions work. An enemy's turn: `advance` returns `enemyTurn` and `game-server.ts` runs `runEnemyTurn` after `enemyTurnDelayMs` (a stale turn is ignored): burning ticks, statuses count down, and it acts on the plan its kind's `behavior` gives (`enemies.ts`: `ENEMIES` hound/cultist/keeper, each with armor, speed, vision, carried `light`, hp for the party's size, attacks and `behavior`). The deciding is `ai.ts`, pure and deterministic: `plan(situation, self)` returns a path (within speed) and a deed (an attack on a character, or the Keeper's toll), from a `Situation` (`situationFor` in `engine.ts`: obstacles, the lit mask in the dark, the standing party, who may be walked through, the Bell's cells and whether it was touched) and what the enemy knows of itself (`EnemyState`: `target`, `lastHitBy` (set when a character hits it), `lastSeen`, `post`). An enemy targets only characters it `seenBy`s; with nobody in sight it goes to where it last saw someone (spawned foes start knowing where the nearest character stood). `rush` (the Hound) keeps after its target but turns on whoever hurt it last or someone `SWITCH_BY` steps closer (said to the table); `skirmish` (the Cultist) knifes whoever is beside it, else slings from where it stands or walks until it has a shot, and at `RETREAT_AT` hp or less and cornered falls back to the farthest cell it can still sling from; `guardian` (the Keeper) tolls when characters are within reach and it isn't resting (every turn once the Bell's object is `used`), goes first for anyone within `BELL_GUARD` of the Bell, never walks beyond `LEASH` of its post, and walks home when nobody is on its ground. Outside fights, `AdventureState.sentries` are enemies on the table walking a round of waypoints or standing at a post (`postSentries`, from an encounter's `sentries`: the Hollow's Keeper at `KEEPER_POST` and two cultists on `CULTIST_ROUNDS`, posted when the party arrives); the game server's patrol timer (`patrolMs`, default 1500; 0 off, as in most tests) calls `patrol(room)`, which steps each round (`patrolStep`) and then `detect`s; `afterMove` detects too. When a sentry sees a standing character, its fight starts with every sentry of it joining where it stands ("The Bell Cultist spots The Warden!"). The GM removing every sentry before a fight counts as winning it ("The way is clear."). Saves keep the sentries and each enemy's memory. A fallen enemy leaves the order (`leaveOrder`); removing the combatant whose turn it is passes the turn on. Fights are recorded as `active`/`won`/`lost`; winning posts how many rounds it took and raises `won_well`/`won_chamber`/`won_hollow` (the Hollow's chapter now asks the party to get past the Keeper before finding Tobin). `pendingEnemyTurn` tells the game server to run a saved enemy turn after a load; saves keep the order, whose turn and each enemy's kind and rest, and read saves from before initiative (characters first, then enemies). Limited uses and statuses reset when a fight starts and after it is won. Victory stands the downed (not the dead) back up at 1 HP. ## Platform constraints

- **The frontend is a static SPA.** It uses `adapter-static` with `fallback: 'index.html'`, and `+layout.ts` sets `ssr = false` and `prerender = false`. It has no SvelteKit server routes, hooks, or form actions. The same bundle ships in the Tauri and Capacitor shells. Anything needing a secret, direct DB access, Redis, or authoritative game logic goes in `server/` (or behind Supabase), never in `src/`.
- `VITE_*` env values are inlined into the bundle at build time. Only the anon/publishable key belongs there, never the service_role key.
- `src/lib/api.ts` rewrites `localhost` to `10.0.2.2` for the Android emulator. Build URLs from `API_URL` / `GAME_SERVER_URL`, not from `import.meta.env` directly. `GAME_SERVER_URL` defaults to port 8787 on the page's host (localhost inside native shells), overridable with `VITE_GAME_SERVER_URL`. `src/lib/supabase.ts` is the shared Supabase client (unused so far).
- Svelte 5 runes mode is forced for all project files (`vite.config.ts` `compilerOptions.runes`). Use `$state`/`$derived`/`$props`, not legacy `export let`/stores syntax.
- Formatting uses tabs, single quotes, and no trailing commas, with a print width of 100 (`prettier.config.js`).

## Product requirements (from the project brief)

The brief sets behavior, not stack. Extend the existing architecture. Don't add frameworks or services unless a concrete need justifies them.

**Layering.** Domain/game state → networking/sync → client state → 3D rendering + UI. The renderer only visualizes state; it is never the source of truth. Keep domain logic (scenes, tokens, grid, movement, visibility, dice, permissions) in plain, renderer-free modules so it can be unit-tested in the `server` Vitest project and reused by whatever runs authoritatively. Keep system-agnostic: no D&D-specific rules in core concepts. The Hollow Bell's own rules (characters, abilities, enemies, adventure state) live apart from the core grid/visibility/permissions modules, so milestone 36 can extract them into content.

**Authoritative server.** Clients send requests. The server validates identity, role, ownership, movement, scene edits, dice expressions, and persisted data, then applies the change and broadcasts it. The client is never trusted for roles, token ownership, positions, dice results, or permissions. Dice are rolled server-side. Dice parsing must never `eval` input. Scene data, chat, and assets must not carry executable content.

**Core concepts.** The domain distinguishes: Room (GM + players), Scene, Scene Object (floor/wall/door/prop/light), Token, Player, Role (GM / player / spectator), and **Grid Position (logical) vs World Position (render)**, which are kept separate. Assets are reusable resources referenced by scene objects, and placement is stored separately.

**Permissions (MVP defaults).** The GM sees everything, moves any token, edits the scene, controls lights, and reveals areas. A player sees allowed areas, moves only owned tokens, rolls dice, and chats. A spectator views, rolls dice, and chats. Enforcement happens server-side, not only in the UI.

**State split.** Shared authoritative state (players, tokens, scene, objects, lights, visibility) is kept separate from local UI state (selection, active tool, camera mode, hovered cell, drag). Don't keep competing copies of shared state.

**Subsystems (MVP level).**

- Square grid only, but behind an abstraction: cell size, dimensions, snapping, highlight, distance.
- Grid-based movement, not physics.
- Tactical and tabletop camera modes. Camera code stays out of gameplay rules.
- Visibility is grid-based: vision radius plus GM reveal, with visible, explored, and hidden states.
- Basic ambient, directional, and point/torch lighting.
- Dice support d4 through d100 and `NdX+M` expressions.
- Room chat carries name, content, timestamp, and type, plus system messages.
- A simple GM scene editor handles select, move, rotate, scale, delete, and creating tokens, objects, walls, doors, and lights. It is not a modeling tool. Primitive placeholder geometry is fine.

**Persistence.** Save plain domain data, never renderer objects. Serialized scenes are **versioned** so the schema can evolve. Reconnecting clients must recover their membership, identity, current scene, and owned-token access.

**Out of MVP scope.** Out of MVP scope: character sheets, a combat system, full rules, marketplace, billing, a complex animation system, procedural generation, advanced 3D line-of-sight, voice/video, campaign management, and mobile-first UX. Don't build these early. The post-MVP roadmap brings some of them in for The Hollow Bell (its four characters, Combat 1.0, cinematic moments). Build them only as far as that adventure needs; the others stay out of scope.

**Performance.** Performance is first-class: no per-frame rebuilds for small changes, instancing for repeated geometry, lazy asset loading, and no high-frequency network traffic for visual-only state. Profile before optimizing. Target modern desktop browsers without requiring bleeding-edge APIs.

**Required test coverage.** Tests must cover grid conversion, movement/distance, dice parsing and results, permission validation, scene serialization, room join/leave, and multiplayer sync of movement, dice, and chat. One automated test must specifically prove **a player cannot move a token they don't control**.

**Workflow.** Build in small vertical milestones. Each one should be runnable and pass `check`, `lint`, `test`, and `build`. Milestones 1 (join a room, see the table and players), 2 (tokens and movement), 3 (dice and chat), 4 (walls and doors), 5 (fog of war) and 6 (save/load) are complete, which completes the MVP flow below; 7 (lighting), 8 (props and the object editor), 9 (3D dice) and 10 (Supabase scene storage) are complete too. The roadmap is tracked in GitHub issues. #4 is the overall tracking issue. #24 tracks the post-MVP roadmap (milestones 11–40, one sub-issue each, milestone N = issue #N+14), whose source text is `docs/ROADMAP.md`. Milestones 11 (The Hollow Bell vertical slice, #25), 12 (character gameplay, #26), 13 (interaction system, #27), 14 (adventure state machine, #28), 15 (Bellweather Village, #29), 16 (investigation system, #30), 17 (The Monastery, #31), 18 (advanced 3D interaction, #32), 19 (Combat 1.0, #33), 20 (Enemy AI, #34) and 21 (Visibility 2.0, #35) are done; next is 22, Lighting as gameplay (#36). Elevation is a height field (one level per cell), not stacked storeys: nothing can stand under a balcony. The monastery and the Hollow from milestone 14 are deliberately small first versions for M17 (The Monastery), M23 (The Hollow), M24 (the final boss) and M25 (the ending system) to build out. Close the matching sub-issue when its work merges, and add new work as a sub-issue under the milestone it belongs to.

**MVP done.** The MVP is done when all of the following work without manual code or DB edits:

1. The GM creates a room and shares a join link.
2. A player joins, and both see the same 3D table.
3. The GM places walls, a door, and tokens.
4. The player moves their token and the GM sees it.
5. The player rolls a d20 and both clients see the result.
6. Chat works.
7. The GM hides and reveals areas.
8. The GM saves the scene, and reloading it rebuilds the table.
