# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What this is

thirdfold is a browser-based, real-time multiplayer **3D virtual tabletop (VTT)** for tabletop RPGs. It should feel like a living 3D miniature tabletop, not a 2D VTT with a 3D renderer added on. Built so far: rooms (create/join/reconnect) over an authoritative WebSocket server, a 3D table with the connected players (milestone 1), tokens the GM places/assigns/deletes and owners move on the grid (milestone 2), a shared room log with chat, server-rolled dice and system notices (milestone 3), GM-built walls and doors that block movement (milestone 4), fog of war with token vision and GM reveal/hide (milestone 5), saving/loading scenes (milestone 6), which completes the MVP definition of done below, lighting (milestone 7), props with a select/move/rotate/scale/delete editor (milestone 8), 3D dice (milestone 9), and Supabase-backed scene storage (milestone 10). Post-MVP (`docs/ROADMAP.md`), milestone 11 made the opening section of the built-in adventure **The Hollow Bell** playable end to end: the GM starts it, players pick one of four characters, explore Bellweather, talk to an NPC, find a clue, fight a Hollow Hound turn by turn, and walk out to end the section. Milestone 12 made the four characters playable RPG characters: stats, two actions each (a basic attack and a limited ability), status effects, downed and death states, a character sheet and introduction, and GM overrides. Milestone 13 added world objects with states (hidden, visible, interactable, used, disabled, destroyed, moved, opened, closed, lit, unlit) that players change by interacting and the GM can set directly. Milestone 14 made The Hollow Bell a whole story driven by a state machine: nine chapters (village → discover the bell → the monastery gate → the nave → the hidden chamber → the bell rings → descend → the Hollow → the final decision; twelve since M24 added the pit, the waking and the ringing before the decision, and thirteen with M25's `the_descent`, a branch off the decision) across three tables (Bellweather, the monastery, the Hollow), moved on by play alone (talking, finding things, winning fights, walking into places, answering choices), tracking events, clues, NPC states, decisions, fights, defeated enemies, object states and the ending, and saved with the table (scene file v4). Milestone 15 built Bellweather out into a whole village to investigate (the square and well, the Tolling Rest, the Hale house, Widow Crane's cottage, the smithy, the chapel of Saint Agna, the churchyard, the gated mountain path and the pilgrims' arch at its foot) with ten villagers who talk, hand out clues, change state, react to what the party does nearby and take cover from the Hound, plus flickering flames and drifting mist after dusk. Milestone 16 made investigation physical: characters examine, inspect (read), search and interact with things, and listen and observe wherever they stand; some of it takes a server-rolled check (d20 + a stat against a difficulty, one try per character), and what a character finds is evidence (a document, an object, a trace or testimony) known only to them and the GM until their player shares it, while what people say is heard by all; evidence the party knows raises story events that unlock objectives, and people react to what the character talking to them knows. Milestone 17 gave tables elevation and made the monastery thirdfold's 3D showcase: each cell has a level, so there are balconies, ledges, a bell tower and stairs between them; movement climbs one level a step; sight runs in 3D (over walls from high enough, hidden by rising ground) and through windows; players on different levels see each other; and when the party enters the nave the tower bell swings on its chains, dust falls, the table shudders and something huge passes far below. Milestone 18 made interaction physical: characters push, pull, open, close, pick up, drop, rotate, activate, destroy, move and trigger things; pushed and pulled props slide a cell (never through a wall, a token or over a drop), items are carried from table to table and put down anywhere, and a mechanism is a chain of changes the server plays out step by step (pulling the ringing chamber's lever swings it down, runs the chain up the wall, then grinds the grate open, which moves the story on), shown to every client with the same motions and synthesized sounds. Milestone 19 made combat a full loop: when a fight starts everyone rolls initiative on the server, turns go one at a time in that order (a character moves up to its speed and acts once, then ends its turn; each enemy acts on its own turn after a pause), a tracker and an arrow over the token show whose turn it is, and there are three kinds of enemy: the Hollow Hound (rushes and bites), the Bell Cultist (slings from range, knifes up close) and the Bell Keeper (tough, hammers, and tolls its bell to hurt and slow everyone near it), with a new fight against the Keeper and two cultists in the Hollow. Milestone 20 gave the enemies reliable AI, on the server in and out of fights: they only see what the rules let them see (vision, walls, and in the dark only lit cells or right beside them), the Hollow is dark and watched (cultists walk their rounds carrying lanterns, the Keeper stands guard by the Bell) and a fight begins when one of them spots a character; in a fight the Hound chases what it sees, keeps after its prey but turns on whoever hurt it or someone much closer, and runs to where it last saw someone; the Cultist slings from range, knifes up close and falls back when badly hurt; the Keeper never strays far from its post, goes first for anyone near the Bell, tolls when crowded, and tolls every turn once someone has laid hands on the Bell. Milestone 21 made visibility a gameplay system: each player sees only through their own tokens unless the GM shares the party's sight, the GM reveals or hides a whole walled room with one click, a character who walks into a room learns its whole layout (not who stands in it), the GM can hide any token or prop from the players (fog or not), and what each player has discovered is saved with the table and given back by name. Milestone 22 made light a matter of gameplay: a table can have dark areas where only light lets anyone see, even by day (the ringing chamber is one, lit by a single candle stub), characters light and put out torches (in a fight too, as their action), some things are only there while a torch burns (the ringers' carvings in the chamber, a cleft in the Hollow's rock that seals when the cultists' torch goes out), and the Bell's note lights the whole Hollow for a moment (when it is first touched, when the Keeper tolls, and when the chamber's bell rings), for the party and the watch alike. Milestone 23 rebuilt the Hollow as the final environment, a cavern far larger than the monastery (48×36): a black lake running off into the void, a causeway across it to an island walled in ancient stone where the giant Bell hangs in its frame among gears and chains over the pit, the ruins of a chapel on the western shore with a narrow bridge to a ledge by the island's wall (in through the cleft), and terraces climbing the eastern wall to the Steps and the Watch, a shelf of statues with a high bridge down to the island's east arch. Arriving, the Bell sounds and its flash shows the party the whole cavern, whose shape they then remember. Milestone 24 made the Hollow itself the final encounter, in four phases played as chapters: the party discovers it (looking into the pit, unless they already saw its eye), survives its waking (tendrils rise from the pit while the floor cracks under the party and heaves each round), takes hold of the Bell as it starts ringing itself (it hurts, flashes and calls up more tendrils every round nobody pulls its rope; three pulls hold it), and chooses what becomes of it: destroy it (the Hollow's Hand attacks, and only surviving it ends the story), silence it (the Hollow begins to awaken) or use it (speak with the Hollow). Earlier play changes the encounter: a party that already saw the eye skips the pit, a Bell touched before brings one more tendril, Tobin freed with Pell's name helps pull, the hand bell answers the Bell as a pull from anywhere, knowing the ringers' rule shows the way and changes what the Hollow makes of the party, and the promise to Oswin changes a choice and the ending's coda. Milestone 25 made the endings a system of three: Silence (the Bell destroyed, after surviving the Hollow's Hand, or silenced for good), Descent (the party goes down into the pit, a new table, and stills the Hollow's Heart) and Communion (the party rings the Bell and speaks with it); each has its own narration, final scene (the table changes: the Bell broken and every light out, the Bell dark with the eye red below, the whole Hollow lit a calm blue, the Heart still and grey), result (what became of the Bell, the Hollow, Tobin and Bellweather) and completion: the story is over, the table is shown whole to everyone, and nothing in it moves or acts any more. Milestone 26 let the GM direct the adventure instead of building it: a Direct panel pauses the game (players can't move or act, enemies and mechanisms wait), skips to the next scene (winning the fight at hand first), starts any of the story's fights where the party is and ends one as won or calls it off, brings on enemies (joining the fight at hand in initiative order, or standing guard until they spot someone and start the GM's own fight) and removes them, picks people to move, makes any story event happen, sets the time of day and switches lights, reveals and hides areas and rooms, and rolls dice in the open or in secret; with these alone a GM runs The Hollow Bell from start to finish. Milestone 27 made a new player's first session self-explanatory: the invite link asks only for a name ("Join the game"; watching is a link below), then the player chooses a character, is welcomed to where the party is (the server's words, with their first goal), and learns to play by doing it: an interactive tutorial asks them to walk, talk to someone or touch something, look around, open their character sheet and say something or roll, ticking each off as they do it (in any order), then "Start" hands them over to the story; a step indicator shows where they are throughout. Milestone 28 turned that tutorial into onboarding inside the real session, teaching thirdfold's core interaction by using it: move your miniature, look around (Observe notices a faint blue glint up the road), walk up to the glow (marked on the table with a column of light), inspect it, and you've discovered something (a child's tin bell, private evidence, shown with how evidence and sharing work); then talk to someone, open your sheet and say something at the table. Milestone 29 gave thirdfold its sound, all synthesized in the browser and derived from what each client already receives (no audio traffic): ambience for each place and hour (wind and crickets over the village at dusk, the monastery's cold draughts, the Hollow's drone and dripping lake, the Heart beating), footsteps on earth, stone or flesh, doors, dice, combat (hits heavy and light, misses, healing, guarding, fire, the fallen), UI (a click, your turn, a clue, a choice, a new chapter, a refused action) and dynamic music whose mood follows the story (the village, the monastery, the Hollow, the Heart; rising for choices, the dark, fights and the finale; resolving at the ending or mourning a fallen party), crossfading between moods; the bell is its voice: one family of bells built from a real bell's partials rings the great bell's toll, its flash, the Keeper's toll, the hand bell, the music's motif and the UI chimes. A Sound menu in the header mutes it or sets the music, ambience and effects levels. Milestone 30 added a few cinematic moments, camera shots a line of narration calls for, each a turn of the head of a few seconds that any drag, click or scroll ends, leaving the camera with the player: the first bell (the camera looks up the mountain path toward the monastery, which the party then remembers), the bell ring (the tower bell as the party enters the nave), a discovery (the door behind Saint Agna), the Hollow (the camera pulls back over the whole cavern in the Bell's light), and the final encounter (the pit, when Tobin is found and when the Hollow wakes). Milestone 31 made sessions survive normal network trouble and restarts: live rooms are kept (in files, or in Supabase when configured) and restored when the game server starts again, so everyone resumes the same seat with their tokens, the log, the table and the story; a dropped client shows a reconnecting banner (a countdown, the attempt, Try now) and retries at once when the network or the tab comes back; when the GM's connection drops mid-story the game waits for them and carries on when they return; a fight passes the turn of a character whose player has gone after a while; a player who joins a story under way catches up on the ground the party has explored; and the camera view is remembered across a refresh. Milestone 32 made saving a whole session feature: a GM's saves are theirs on any device, by a lasting GM key the server issues to their browser (which they can copy to another), a story in play saves itself as it goes on, and the landing page greets the GM with their latest game ("The Hollow Bell. Last played: Yesterday 21:43. The quiet village · Bellweather. The Warden (Ana). Continue"); Continue opens a new table on that save with the story where it was (chapter, objectives, characters, evidence, what each player had explored), and a player rejoining under the same name gets their character back. Milestone 33 gave the session an end screen: when the story ends, won or lost, everyone sees a headline for the ending ("The Bell is silent"), how the party came through ("The party survived.", "Two of the three survived."), "Adventure complete", who played whom, the time played, the ending, what came of it, the choices made and a tally (chapters, fights won, foes defeated, evidence found); Return to table closes it to keep exploring (a Summary button brings it back), Replay starts the story over with the same party (the GM; a player asks the GM, and everyone sees who would like to play again), and Return to lobby goes back to the landing page. Milestone 34 was a performance pass driven by measurements (`docs/PERFORMANCE.md`: how to measure, what was found, what changed): the server keeps each sight (a token's vision, a light's reach) while the table's obstacles stay the same, so a sync after a move costs a fifth of what it did (the Hollow's view work 10.7 → 2.1 ms with seven viewers, a patrol step 14.3 → 1.5 ms); three.js and the renderer are their own chunk, loaded when a table shows and prefetched on the landing page and join form (the room page 196 → 36 kB gzipped; the join form on slow 4G 1.8 → 1.3 s); the sun's shadows are redrawn only when something on the table changed (a camera move draws about 40% fewer calls), the sun keeps casting in the dark so changing the time of day no longer recompiles every shader, and light is worked out once per frame however many updates came together; a `?perf` overlay shows what rendering costs. Milestone 35 gave thirdfold a repeatable asset pipeline (`docs/ASSETS.md`), with The Hollow Bell as its reference content: sources in `assets/` (models of props, characters, NPCs and enemies made from primitive parts or provided as meshes-only glTF, materials, texture recipes or PNGs, environments that dress a place's floor, ground, walls and table, and sounds rendered from the bell family or provided as WAV/Ogg) build into content-hashed files in `static/assets/` and a checked manifest; every prop, figure and place of the story now comes from it (the village on grass under plaster walls, the monastery's flagstones, the cavern's rock, twelve villagers on seven figures tinted by their colour, the four characters and every enemy as figures), loaded lazily with placeholders until each arrives, and the great and hand bells ring from rendered samples. Milestone 36 made content authoring a structure (`docs/ADVENTURES.md`): an adventure is data (`AdventureDef`: scenes, characters, NPCs, enemies, objects, encounters, events, dialogues, objectives and endings, whose happenings are effects chosen by rules with conditions, the way NPC lines always worked), the engine runs whichever adventure a table plays and knows none of them, The Hollow Bell is written in that structure and runs from it, a validator names every reference that goes nowhere, and a second, tiny adventure plays start to finish on the same engine in the tests. Milestone 37 began user-built tables: the GM starts a new, empty table of a chosen size and look from the Scene panel, paints floors cell by cell or area by area (stone, wood, grass, dirt, sand, water, or off the map, where nobody can stand), and alongside walls, doors, props, ground, lights, fog and tokens, saves it, or shares it: Share table gives a link that opens a copy of the table (the world, without the story or who played whom) for any GM, from the landing page or pasted into the Scene panel. Milestone 38 let creators build adventures without code: an adventure file is the content structure as plain JSON (tables as scene files, hit points as numbers, characters from the library), checked in full by the server, and the builder at `/builder` edits one section by section (scenes, the flow of chapters and objectives, events, people and dialogue, enemies and encounters, things with verbs and rules, trigger areas, clues, choices as branches, rewards, endings), shows what's wrong as you go, saves and opens files, starts from an example (The Miller's Key), and plays it: Play it opens a table with the adventure, and a save carries the adventure with it. Milestone 39 proved the structure is general with a second adventure, **The Last Train to Blackwater**, a supernatural western mystery aboard a night train (Arizona, 1889) written entirely as an adventure file and built in through the same loader: seven chapters over three tables (the train's four cars end to end, the locomotive, the ghost town of Blackwater), six passengers to question, a vanished man, a frost trail, a key carried to the baggage car and its strongbox, the coffins' dead, midnight (the lamps go out and the dead walk the aisles), the dead engineer at the throttle and three endings (Stopped Short, Laid to Rest, End of the Line); the GM picks it from the Adventure panel's catalog of the server's adventures, and its characters are introduced in its own words. Milestone 40 began thirdfold as a platform: creators publish adventures from the builder to a library (`/library`) under a creator name, by their GM key, each publish a new version (games under way keep theirs), and manage them (take one out of the library, put it back, remove it); any GM searches the library by title, story or creator, orders it by rating, plays or date, opens a creator's page, and runs an adventure for their table (from the library or the Adventure panel, which credits its creator); everyone who played it can rate it 1-5 stars once the story is over, and the library counts plays; and a GM can list their game (Invite only / Open to all) so anyone finds it among the front page's open games and joins or watches.

## Commands

```bash
npm install
cp .env.example .env        # set VITE_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY from `npx supabase start` output
npm run db:start            # local Supabase (API :54321, Postgres :54322, Studio :54323); db:stop
npm run cache:up            # Redis :6379 via docker compose; cache:down
npm run server              # authoritative game server, ws://localhost:8787 (tsx watch); saves scenes to ./data/scenes (SCENES_DIR), or to Supabase with SUPABASE_URL + SUPABASE_SERVICE_KEY
npm run dev                 # web on http://localhost:1420 (strictPort)

npm run check               # svelte-check for the app + `tsc -p server` for the game server
npm run lint                # prettier --check + eslint
npm run format              # prettier --write
npm test                    # all vitest projects, run once
npm run build               # static SPA into build/
```

Tests are split into two Vitest projects in `vite.config.ts`:

- `server` runs in Node and picks up `src/**/*.{test,spec}.ts` plus `server/**/*.{test,spec}.ts`. Put pure domain logic tests here (grid, dice, permissions, serialization). `server/game-server.spec.ts` starts a real server on port 0 and drives it with `ws` clients; extend it for every new multiplayer action.
- `client` runs in headless Chromium through Playwright and picks up `src/**/*.svelte.{test,spec}.ts`, the component tests.

Run a subset:

```bash
npx vitest run --project server
npx vitest run src/lib/path/to/file.spec.ts
npx vitest run -t "test name"
```

`expect.requireAssertions` is on, so a test with no assertions fails. The live Supabase tests in `server/supabase-scene-store.spec.ts` are skipped unless `SUPABASE_URL`, `SUPABASE_SERVICE_KEY` and `SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` are set; to run them, start local Supabase (`npm run db:start`, which needs a Docker daemon), apply migrations (`npx supabase migration up`), and export the values from `npx supabase status -o env` (`API_URL`, `SECRET_KEY`, `PUBLISHABLE_KEY`). The `client` project needs a Playwright Chromium that matches the installed `playwright` version.

CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs on every pull request and on pushes to `main`, with two jobs. `verify` runs `npm ci`, installs Playwright's Chromium, then `check`, `lint`, `test` (both projects) and `build`. `supabase` starts a local Supabase with the migrations applied (`npx supabase start`, only the services the tests need), exports `SUPABASE_URL`/`SUPABASE_SERVICE_KEY`/`SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` from `supabase status -o env`, and runs `server/supabase-scene-store.spec.ts` (saved scenes, their owners, live rooms, and the adventure library: `libraryStoreSuite` from `server/library-store.suite.ts`, the same suite the memory and file stores pass), failing if any test was skipped. A change that needs new env for tests must wire it into the workflow, or the tests will silently skip there.

Schema changes: `npx supabase migration new <name>`, write SQL in `supabase/migrations/`, then `npx supabase db reset`. This drops local data. RLS is on for new tables, so a table without a policy returns empty results.

Native targets: `npm run desktop` / `desktop:build` builds Tauri. `npm run android` / `ios` builds, runs `cap sync`, and launches Capacitor. `android/` and `ios/` are generated and gitignored. Recreate them with `npx cap add <platform>`. Native builds only pick up web changes after `npm run mobile:sync`.

## Architecture

Three layers, each importable only in one direction:

- **`src/lib/game/`: shared domain + wire protocol.** Plain TypeScript with no DOM, Svelte, three.js or Node APIs, and relative imports only (no `$lib`), because the game server imports it directly. `grid.ts` holds logical grid ↔ world conversions, `token.ts` the token model, `dice.ts` the dice-expression parser/roller (hand-written tokenizer with hard limits, random source injected), `chat.ts` the log entry types plus `/roll` command parsing (`/gmroll` or `/gr`: a secret roll, whose entry carries an `audience`: the GM only, or the roller and the GM), `objects.ts` walls, windows and doors (they run along grid lines between cells, from corner to corner; `GridEdge`/corner helpers live in `grid.ts`; a window is a wall with `window: true`: it blocks movement, not sight; `windowEdges`) plus the pathing rules (`canStep`, `isReachable` BFS, `findPath`/`walkDistance` shortest walks, `cutWall`); these take `Blockers`: either a bare set of blocking wall/door edges, or full `Obstacles` that add `solid` (can't enter) and `opaque` (can't see past, but the cell itself is visible) cell masks, `windows`, and `levels` (each cell's elevation, or null for a flat table: a step may climb or drop at most `MAX_STEP` = 1 level, so stairs are runs of cells one level apart, and a balcony's edge is a drop nobody steps off), `props.ts` the asset catalog (`ASSETS`: name, footprint, what it blocks) and prop placements (asset id, anchor cell, quarter-turn rotation, visual-only scale), `footprintCells`, `placementProblem` (shared by server and editor preview) and `obstaclesFor(grid, objects, props)`, which is what movement, sight and light should use, `visibility.ts` line of sight (cell-to-cell, blocked exactly where movement is; with levels it runs in 3D from the viewer's eye, its level + `EYE_LEVELS`, to just above the target, + `TARGET_LEVELS`: ground rising above the line blocks it, and a line high enough passes over walls (`WALL_LEVELS` above the higher floor beside them) and sight-blocking props (`PROP_LEVELS`); on a flat table this is exactly the flat rule), round vision radius, `sees` (whether something with a vision radius sees a cell: the same radius and line, and in the dark only lit cells or right beside; what enemies detect with), and `CellMask` ↔ base64 bitset encoding for `FogView`, `lights.ts` the room's ambient level (`day`/`dusk`/`dark`), GM light sources and token-carried light (`lightSources`, `litMask` for the rules, `lightLevels` for rendering; light spreads exactly like sight), dark areas (`withDarkness`; `seenByLight` is the rule: where it is dark, everywhere after dark or in a dark area at any hour, only lit cells can be seen; null means everything counts as lit), `floor.ts` floors (`FLOORS`: plain (the table's own surface, 0), stone, wood, grass, dirt, sand, water, and `void` (`VOID`): off the map; `FloorMap` one byte per cell, `withFloor` (null once all plain), `encodeFloor`/`decodeFloor`, `knownFloor`; `obstaclesFor(grid, objects, props, levels, floor)` makes void cells solid, not opaque), `terrain.ts` the level map (`LevelMap`, one byte per cell, 0 to `MAX_LEVEL`; `withLevel` sets an area and returns null once all flat; base64 `encodeLevels`/`decodeLevels`; `knownLevels` masks it to the cells a viewer has explored), `chat.ts` log entries can carry an `audience` (`'gm'`, or `{ players }`: only those players and the GM read it; `canSeeLogEntry` in `server/views.ts` filters snapshots and broadcasts) and include `check` entries (an investigation check: action, stat, roll, `dc`, success; the client throws the d20); narration may carry a `cue` (`'toll'`), a cinematic moment clients play (presentation only), and a `Shot` (`focus` cell or null for the whole table, `frame` close/wide/table): a camera move; `motion.ts` the `Motion` (a prop's `shake`/`swing`/`land` and a `Sound`: clank, rattle, grind, chime, crack, scrape, thud) sent as the `motion` server message, presentation only, and `heardOnly` (what a viewer who can't see the prop gets: the sound alone); `rooms.ts` finds rooms: `roomBoundary(objects)` (every wall, window and door edge, open or shut) and `roomAround(grid, boundary, cell)`, a flood fill that returns the room's cells or null past `MAX_ROOM_CELLS` (open ground); tokens and props may be `hidden` (GM-set; kept from every non-GM viewer, fog or not, except a token's own owner); `scene-file.ts` the versioned save format (currently v8; `migrate` upgrades v1 → v2 (lights) → v3 (props) → v4 (`adventure`: the state of a story played at the table, a `SavedStory` `{ id, version, state }` that the core only checks is bounded plain JSON; the adventure module validates the contents) → v5 (`terrain`: the level map as base64, or null for flat; windows on walls) → v6 (`fog.shared`, and `discovery`: each player's explored mask by name) → v7 (`darkness`: the dark areas as a base64 mask, or null) → v8 (`environment`: how the table looks, an environment asset's id, or null; tokens may carry `model`, a model asset's id) → v9 (`floor`: what each cell is made of, a base64 `FloorMap`, or null)); `blankScene(name, width, height, environment)` is a new empty table, and `sharedScene` a table as shared (no story, no discoveries, no token owners) (`serializeScene`, and `parseSceneFile`, which migrates old versions forward and validates every field; anything read from disk or uploaded goes through it), and `permissions.ts` the role/ownership rules (`canMoveToken`, `canEditScene`, `canUseDoor`: GM always, a player only with an owned token beside the door) that the server enforces and the UI reuses only to decide what to offer. `protocol.ts` defines every `ClientMessage`/`ServerMessage` and `parseClientMessage`, the only way network input becomes typed data.
- **`server/`: the authoritative game server** (Node + `ws`, run with `tsx`, own `server/tsconfig.json`). `rooms.ts` is socket-free room state (`RoomManager`, returns `Result` values with an `ErrorCode`). `scene.ts` holds the token and wall/door actions (`createToken`, `moveToken`, `createObject`, `toggleDoor`, `setTerrain` (GM, message `terrain_set`: an area to a level), `setFloor` (GM, message `floor_set`: an area to a floor; void is refused under a token), …), and `obstacles(room)` (walls, props and `room.terrain`), each taking the acting `Player` and checking permission and validity before mutating. Player moves must be reachable without crossing walls or closed doors; GM moves are exempt. A door placed on a wall cuts that wall (`objects_changed` carries the resulting upserts and removals together). `views.ts` is where fog is enforced: `viewFor(room, viewer)` returns the tokens, objects, `FogView` and ground (`terrain`: `room.terrain`'s levels, and `floor`: `room.floor`, each only for cells the viewer has explored under fog; `terrain_update` and `floor_update` carry changes) a viewer may see (fog off: everything; GM: everything plus the party's visibility for shading; player: own tokens' vision (the whole party's when `fog.shared`, set by the GM with `fog_share`) ∪ GM-revealed cells, where it is dark (`dark` ambient, or `room.darkness`, set by the GM with `darkness_set`) a token only sees cells that some light reaches (plus its own cell), computed once per sync in `sceneContext` from `lightFor(room)` in `scene.ts`; every sight (a token's vision, a light's reach) comes from the room's `SightCache` (`visibility.ts`; `room.sights`, `sightsFor(room, blocked)` in `scene.ts`, also used for the enemies' light): kept across syncs while the obstacles are the same by content, cleared when they change, so a sync after a move works out one sight (null while `room.flashUntil` is in the future: a flash lights everything, and the game server syncs again when it fades); views carry `darkness` masked to the cells the viewer has explored (`darkness_update`); plus remembered `explored` cells, which get walls, props and light fixtures but not tokens, and a token standing in a walled room marks the whole room explored (its layout, via `SceneContext.rooms`); `fogRoom` (message `fog_room`) reveals or hides the room around a cell, refusing open ground; spectator: the whole party's vision). `game-server.ts` never broadcasts scene changes directly: after each scene action `syncRoom` recomputes every connected viewer's view and sends only `diffView` against what that socket was last sent (`sentViews`), so hidden tokens, walls and their ids never reach a client (a test inspects raw frames for this). Log entries can carry `audience: 'gm'` (notices naming GM-only tokens) and are filtered per viewer too. Scenes: `scene-io.ts` exports a room to a scene file and applies one back (replacing grid/tokens/walls/fog, re-matching token owners by id then by player name, restoring each player's explored map by lowercase name from `discovery`, else empty); `scene-store.ts` stores them by unguessable 128-bit id (`FileSceneStore`, atomic write-then-rename, ids validated before touching paths; `MemorySceneStore` is the default for tests); `supabase-scene-store.ts` is the Postgres-backed store (table `public.scenes` from `supabase/migrations`, RLS on with no policies or grants for `anon`/`authenticated`, so only the server's service/secret key can touch it). `server/index.ts` picks Supabase when both `SUPABASE_URL` and `SUPABASE_SERVICE_KEY` are set, else files; setting only one is an error. Save/load/import/export are GM-only, rate-limited, async, and report storage failures as `persistence_failed`. A load sends every viewer a fresh `room_reset` snapshot and restarts their diffs. New adventure action: message in `protocol.ts`, rule in `engine.ts` (tests in `engine.spec.ts`; the story machine, the whole playthrough and saves in `story.spec.ts`; the village and its people in `village.spec.ts`; investigation, private evidence and sharing in `investigation.spec.ts`; pushing, pulling, carrying, turning, breaking and mechanisms in `physical.spec.ts`; dark areas, torches, what only light shows and the flash in `lighting.spec.ts`; the Hollow's levels, bridges, routes and arrival in `hollow.spec.ts`; the finale's phases, the reactions to earlier play and its outcomes in `finale.spec.ts`; the three endings, their final scenes, results and saves in `endings.spec.ts`; initiative, turns, the three enemy kinds, sentries and detection in the dark Hollow in `combat.spec.ts`; the AI's plans in `ai.spec.ts`; the monastery's heights and sight lines in `monastery.spec.ts`; the core elevation rules in `src/lib/game/elevation.spec.ts`), case in `handleAdventure`, and a wire test in the "The Hollow Bell over the wire" block. The renderer reframes the camera whenever the table size changes (its distance haze and far plane grow with tables wider than the Hollow, `FOG`), and the first tokens on a new table snap into place (`TokenLayer.sync` with `snap`) instead of gliding in from the last one.

Identity and reconnection: the welcome message carries a secret 64-hex `sessionToken` (never broadcast; tests assert this; not to be confused with game tokens). The client stores it in `localStorage` under `thirdfold:session:<roomId>` and, after any drop, reconnects with `resume` using exponential backoff (`attempt` and `retryAt` are exposed; `retryNow` tries at once, as the browser coming back `online` or the tab becoming visible does); `RoomView` shows a reconnecting banner (countdown, attempt, Try now) over the last snapshot, a banner when the GM's seat is disconnected, and remembers the camera view in `localStorage` (`thirdfold:view`). Resuming a seat from a second socket closes the older one with code `4001` (`CLOSE_SESSION_REPLACED`, duplicated in the client). The room creator is the only GM; `join` accepts only `player`/`spectator`. The landing page hands its already-seated connection to `/room/[id]` via `handOff`/`takeHandoff`, so navigation doesn't re-handshake.

## Platform constraints

- **The frontend is a static SPA.** It uses `adapter-static` with `fallback: 'index.html'`, and `+layout.ts` sets `ssr = false` and `prerender = false`. It has no SvelteKit server routes, hooks, or form actions. The same bundle ships in the Tauri and Capacitor shells. Anything needing a secret, direct DB access, Redis, or authoritative game logic goes in `server/` (or behind Supabase), never in `src/`.
- `VITE_*` env values are inlined into the bundle at build time. Only the anon/publishable key belongs there, never the service_role key.
- `src/lib/api.ts` rewrites `localhost` to `10.0.2.2` for the Android emulator. Build URLs from `API_URL` / `GAME_SERVER_URL`, not from `import.meta.env` directly. `GAME_SERVER_URL` defaults to port 8787 on the page's host (localhost inside native shells), overridable with `VITE_GAME_SERVER_URL`. `src/lib/supabase.ts` is the shared Supabase client (unused so far).
- Svelte 5 runes mode is forced for all project files (`vite.config.ts` `compilerOptions.runes`). Use `$state`/`$derived`/`$props`, not legacy `export let`/stores syntax.
- Formatting uses tabs, single quotes, and no trailing commas, with a print width of 100 (`prettier.config.js`).

## Product requirements (from the project brief)

The brief sets behavior, not stack. Extend the existing architecture. Don't add frameworks or services unless a concrete need justifies them.

**Layering.** Domain/game state → networking/sync → client state → 3D rendering + UI. The renderer only visualizes state; it is never the source of truth. Keep domain logic (scenes, tokens, grid, movement, visibility, dice, permissions) in plain, renderer-free modules so it can be unit-tested in the `server` Vitest project and reused by whatever runs authoritatively. Keep system-agnostic: no D&D-specific rules in core concepts. The Hollow Bell's own rules (characters, abilities, enemies, adventure state) live apart from the core grid/visibility/permissions modules, so milestone 36 can extract them into content.

**Authoritative server.** Clients send requests. The server validates identity, role, ownership, movement, scene edits, dice expressions, and persisted data, then applies the change and broadcasts it. The client is never trusted for roles, token ownership, positions, dice results, or permissions. Dice are rolled server-side. Dice parsing must never `eval` input. Scene data, chat, and assets must not carry executable content.

**Core concepts.** The domain distinguishes: Room (GM + players), Scene, Scene Object (floor/wall/door/prop/light), Token, Player, Role (GM / player / spectator), and **Grid Position (logical) vs World Position (render)**, which are kept separate. Assets are reusable resources referenced by scene objects, and placement is stored separately.

**Permissions (MVP defaults).** The GM sees everything, moves any token, edits the scene, controls lights, and reveals areas. A player sees allowed areas, moves only owned tokens, rolls dice, and chats. A spectator views, rolls dice, and chats. Enforcement happens server-side, not only in the UI.

**State split.** Shared authoritative state (players, tokens, scene, objects, lights, visibility) is kept separate from local UI state (selection, active tool, camera mode, hovered cell, drag). Don't keep competing copies of shared state.

**Subsystems (MVP level).**

- Square grid only, but behind an abstraction: cell size, dimensions, snapping, highlight, distance.
- Grid-based movement, not physics.
- Tactical and tabletop camera modes. Camera code stays out of gameplay rules.
- Visibility is grid-based: vision radius plus GM reveal, with visible, explored, and hidden states.
- Basic ambient, directional, and point/torch lighting.
- Dice support d4 through d100 and `NdX+M` expressions.
- Room chat carries name, content, timestamp, and type, plus system messages.
- A simple GM scene editor handles select, move, rotate, scale, delete, and creating tokens, objects, walls, doors, and lights. It is not a modeling tool. Primitive placeholder geometry is fine.

**Persistence.** Save plain domain data, never renderer objects. Serialized scenes are **versioned** so the schema can evolve. Reconnecting clients must recover their membership, identity, current scene, and owned-token access.

**Out of MVP scope.** Out of MVP scope: character sheets, a combat system, full rules, marketplace, billing, a complex animation system, procedural generation, advanced 3D line-of-sight, voice/video, campaign management, and mobile-first UX. Don't build these early. The post-MVP roadmap brings some of them in for The Hollow Bell (its four characters, Combat 1.0, cinematic moments). Build them only as far as that adventure needs; the others stay out of scope.

**Performance.** Performance is first-class: no per-frame rebuilds for small changes, instancing for repeated geometry, lazy asset loading, and no high-frequency network traffic for visual-only state. Profile before optimizing. Target modern desktop browsers without requiring bleeding-edge APIs.

**Required test coverage.** Tests must cover grid conversion, movement/distance, dice parsing and results, permission validation, scene serialization, room join/leave, and multiplayer sync of movement, dice, and chat. One automated test must specifically prove **a player cannot move a token they don't control**.

**Workflow.** Build in small vertical milestones. Each one should be runnable and pass `check`, `lint`, `test`, and `build`. Milestones 1 (join a room, see the table and players), 2 (tokens and movement), 3 (dice and chat), 4 (walls and doors), 5 (fog of war) and 6 (save/load) are complete, which completes the MVP flow below; 7 (lighting), 8 (props and the object editor), 9 (3D dice) and 10 (Supabase scene storage) are complete too.
